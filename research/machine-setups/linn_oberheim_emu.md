# Linn, Oberheim and E-mu drum machines: hardware setup, pack contents and famous beats

Machines covered: Linn LM-1, LinnDrum (plus the "Akai Linndrum" folder), Linn 9000, Oberheim DMX, Oberheim DX, E-mu Drumulator, E-mu SP-12, E-mu Modular System. Panel order convention used below: row-based panels are read top row then bottom row, left to right; column-based panels (DMX, DX, LM-1) are read column by column, left to right, top to bottom inside each column.

## 1. What voices does each machine have, in front-panel order, with the exact labels?

### Takeaway
The original owner's manuals fully document the panels of the Oberheim DMX, Oberheim DX and E-mu Drumulator. The LM-1 and LinnDrum layouts can only be partly rebuilt from Kenton's MIDI-retrofit manuals. No pad-by-pad order was found for the Linn 9000, and no default pad assignment for the SP-12. The E-mu Modular has no fixed drum voices.

### Cited Findings

#### Linn LM-1 (1980)
- 12 voices: bass drum, snare, high-hat, high and low toms, tambourine, clave, cabasa, high and low congas, cowbell, handclaps. A 13th mixer channel is the programmable metronome. — [E&MM review by Warren Cann, Sept 1981](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-linn-lm-1-drum-computer/3566)
- The same 12 (kick, snare, hi-hat, cabasa, tambourine, two toms, two congas, cowbell, claves, hand claps). No cymbals, because long samples cost too much at the time. Announced 1979, released 1980, 525 built. — [Wikipedia: Linn LM-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_LM-1)
- There are five accent buttons, duplicating bass drum, snare, hi-hat, cabasa and tambourine. — [E&MM 1981](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-linn-lm-1-drum-computer/3566)
- Early LM-1 (volume control top left): two rows of nine buttons. The top row runs "bass thru clave", with snare 2nd, cabasa 5th and clave 9th. The bottom row reads left to right: BASS, SNARE, HIHAT, HIHAT>, CABASA, TAMB, tom lo, cong lo, cowbell. — [Kenton LM-1 early-version retrofit manual](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN06005-20250619.pdf)
- Later LM-1 (volume control top centre): the top row starts CONGA▲, TOM▲, with hihat 5th and cabasa 9th. The bottom row reads left to right: CONGA▼, TOM▼, SNARE, BASS, HI-HAT, COWBELL, CLAVE, TAMB, CABASA. — [Kenton LM-1 later-version retrofit manual](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN16002-20250619.pdf)
- Kenton's note list writes the soft and loud versions as "bass & BASS", "snare & SNARE", "hi-hat & HI-HAT", "cabasa & CABASA", "tamb & TAMB". "HI-HAT >" (open hat) is a separate entry. — [Kenton LM-1 later](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN16002-20250619.pdf)
- Conflict: an emulation manual lists a "RimShot" voice and no clave. — [vLinn LM-1 manual (Aly James)](https://www.alyjameslab.com/exclusive/VLINN_Linn_LM-1Drums_USER_MANUAL_V2.pdf). E&MM, Wikipedia and Kenton all list a clave.
- Samples were mostly played by session drummer Art Wood. The handclaps are by Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers. — [Wikipedia: Linn LM-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_LM-1)

#### LinnDrum (often called LM-2), 1982
- 15 sounds and 15 hard plastic "pads"; 1982–1985; US$2,995; about 5,000 sold; 8-bit samples at 28–35 kHz. — [Wikipedia: LinnDrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinnDrum)
- Sound list (16 when the hi-hat is counted as open and closed): bass drum, snare drum, sidestick, open hi-hat, closed hi-hat, hi tom, mid tom, lo tom, crash cymbal, ride cymbal, cabasa, tambourine, hi conga, lo conga, cowbell, claps. — [LinnDrum sample-disk README, H. Tangeraas 1994](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/linndrum/00README.TXT)
- Button names from Kenton's Mk2/3 retrofit manual:
  - The top row of eight buttons runs "Hi-Hat1 thru Ride 2". Hi-Hat 2 is the 2nd button and 'Mid Tom' the 5th.
  - A 'Percussion' button with a red LED is the bottom-right button.
  - The bottom-row buttons are named in this order: 'Sidestick', 'Snare 1', 'Snare 2', 'Snare 3', 'Bass 1', 'Bass 2', 'Crash', then Percussion.
  - The same document is inconsistent about whether Snare 1 is bottom-left or bottom-right.
  - Source: [Kenton LinnDrum Mk2/3 retrofit manual](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN26000-20250619.pdf)
- Level variants on the panel: Bass 1&2, Snare 1,2&3, Hi-Hat 1&2, Ride 1&2, Cabasa 1&2, Tamb 1&2. Sidestick levels 1–2, Hi-Hat Open 1 and Crash 1 are "only available when playing from MIDI IN" (via the retrofit). — [Kenton LinnDrum Mk2/3](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN26000-20250619.pdf)

#### Linn 9000 (1984)
- 18 sounds: bass, snare, sidestick, hihat, two crash cymbals, two ride cymbals, four toms, cabasa, tambourine, high and low congas, cowbell, clap. There are 18 velocity- and pressure-sensitive 1.25" rubber pads. List price $5,000 ($7,000 fully expanded); about 1,100 made. — [Wikipedia: Linn 9000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_9000) (citing the Total Trash spec sheet)
- The pads sit in three rows, taking about two-thirds of the panel. Two hi-hat decay controls sit to the left of the pads. Each row has Erase, Repeat and Tap Tempo buttons. — [ES&CM review by Curtis Schwartz, May 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-beating-heart/3428)
- The four tom pads sit in a circle, angled outward. The reviewer counts only 13 real sounds: one Tom, one Ride and one Crash sample, with the extra pads being different tunings. — [E&MM review by Paul Wiffen, Apr 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/linn-9000/1874)
- Neither review gives the pad-by-pad order.

#### Oberheim DMX (1981)
- Faders left to right: BASS, SNARE, HI-HAT, TOMS (spans two faders), CYMBAL, PERC 1, PERC 2, then METRONOME and VOLUME. The drum buttons sit below in 3 rows × 8 columns. The faders set each voice's level in the mixed outputs only. — [DMX Owner's Manual, 3rd ed. June 1982, cover and panel diagram](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- Eight voices, "each voice has three variations, and some of the voices contain two completely different sounds" (DMX manual p.16):
  - BASS: three volume levels.
  - SNARE: three volume levels.
  - HIHAT: closed, accented, and a longer "open" sound.
  - TOM 1: three pitches.
  - TOM 2: lower than TOM 1, three pitches.
  - CYMBAL: a ride, accented or unaccented, plus a crash.
  - PERC 1: tambourine with accent, plus a rimshot.
  - PERC 2: shaker with accent, plus hand claps.
  - Source: [DMX manual p.16](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- Bottom-row buttons, left to right: Bass 3, Snare 3, Open Hihat, Tom 3, Tom 6, Crash, Rimshot, Claps. The toms are numbered Tom 1–6. — [Kenton DMX retrofit manual p.2](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/697116/kenton-dmx-oberheim.html?page=2)
- Rear individual outputs: BASS, SNARE, HI HAT, TOM 1, TOM 2, CYMB, PERC 1, PERC 2. — [DMX manual rear-panel diagram](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- "NUMBER OF SOUNDS: 24" — [DMX manual specs p.32](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf). The 24 sounds come from 11 original samples; 8-bit μ-law PCM. — [Wikipedia: Oberheim DMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberheim_DMX). A 1994 sample-disk README lists 12 distinct samples: bass, snare, open, closed and accented hi-hat, crash, ride, tom, tambourine, rimshot, shaker, claps. — [DMX sample-disk README](https://www-test.nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/dmx/00README.TXT)
- Release year conflict: 1981 per [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/index.html); 1980 per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberheim_DMX).

#### Oberheim DX
- Faders: BASS, SNARE, HI-HAT, TOMS, CYMBALS, PERC, METRONOME, VOLUME. There are 18 drum buttons, three per voice column. Each column reads top to bottom:

  | Column | Top | Middle | Bottom |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | BASS | SOFT | MEDIUM | LOUD |
  | SNARE | SOFT | MEDIUM | LOUD |
  | HI-HAT | CLOSED | ACCENT | OPEN |
  | TOMS | HI | MID | LOW |
  | CYMBALS | RIDE | RIDE 2 | CRASH |
  | PERC | SHAKER 1 | SHAKER 2 | CLAPS |

  Source: [DX Owner's Manual, 2nd ed. Jan 1985, p.3](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf)
- Sound list: BASS with three volume levels; SNARE with three volume levels; HI-HAT closed and accented plus a longer "open"; TOMS with three pitches; CYMBALS: a ride with two volumes, plus a crash; PERC: a shaker with accent, plus hand claps. — [DX manual p.10](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf)
- Specs: Voices 6, Sounds 18. — [DX manual p.38](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf)
- A 1984 review calls the bass/snare placement "somewhat strange". — [ES&CM "Oberheim vs Drumulator", John Webb, Jan 1984](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/oberheim-vs-drumulator/3222)

#### E-mu Drumulator (1983)
- Six Drum/Percussion Sound Select buttons (1–6) with a PERC/DRUMS switch:
  - Upper (PERC) labels: CLAVE, COWBELL, CLAPS, HI HAT OPEN, HI HAT CLOSED, RIDE.
  - Lower (DRUMS) labels: BASS, SNARE, RIM, HI TOM, MID TOM, LO TOM.
  - Buttons 7 and 8 are MET/TRIG and MEM.
  - Four large Drum Play buttons A–D.
  - Source: [Drumulator Owner's Manual §1B–1E panel diagrams](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- At power-on the Drum Play buttons are "A=BASS, B=SNARE, C=COWBELL, D=RIDE". Any of the 12 sounds, or its accented version, can be assigned to any play button. "If you want four snares, you got 'em." — [Drumulator manual §1C, §7A](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Display codes: bA BASS, Sn SNARE, ri RIM, Hi HI TOM, Md MID TOM, Lo LO TOM, CL CLAVE, Cb COWBELL, CP CLAPS, Ho HI HAT OPEN, Hc HI HAT CLOSED, rd RIDE. — [Drumulator manual §2G](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Credits: drums played by Bill Gibson and Chris Pedersen, recorded at Ayre Studios, San Jose. Hardware and software by Dave Rossum. Manual by Craig Anderton. — [Drumulator manual, Appendix D](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Introduced 1983 at US$995. — [Wikipedia: E-mu Drumulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_Drumulator). The manual footer reads "©E-mu Systems, Inc. 1981", which conflicts with the 1983 launch.

#### E-mu SP-12 (1985)
- 8 velocity-sensitive pads and 8 control sliders; "8 voice polyphonic (8 outputs)". — [CCRMA SP-12 project page](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~nolting/220c)
- Hard plastic buttons with piezo-sensed dynamics. The panel shows 8 positions, but up to 32 user samples can be used. Designed 1984, shown as "Drumulator II" in Feb 1985, launched at summer NAMM 1985, replaced by the SP-1200 in Aug 1987. — [Wikipedia: E-mu SP-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-12)
- On the SP-1200, banks A–D give 32 sounds, and the SP-1200 "kept the SP-12's capabilities, inputs, and outputs" (minus cassette and Commodore 1541 support). — [Wikipedia: E-mu SP-1200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-1200)
- 24 ROM sounds: "two bass drum, two snare, two electronic snare, rimshot, cowbell, four toms, four electronic tom, three hi-hat, two clap, two ride cymbal, and crash cymbal". — [Wikipedia: E-mu SP-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-12)
- ROM chip contents:
  - MS1: Electric Snare, Rimshot, Cowbell, Tom.
  - MS2: Bass/Kick Drum, Electric Tom, Hi-Hat, Clap.
  - MS3: Ride, Snare.
  - MS4: Crash.
  - "Some of the sounds are actually one sound transposed to generate variations (such as the toms)."
  - Source: [r-massive "SP12 Sound ROM Hacking"](https://zine.r-massive.com/?p=5771)

#### E-mu Modular System
- A hand-built modular synthesizer. Customers chose modules from a list, for example "Quad Inverter", "Sample-and-Hold" and "Dual Reverb". Designed by Dave Rossum and Scott Wedge. The History section gives production from 1972; the lead says it was built in 1974. There are no fixed drum voices. — [Wikipedia: E-mu Modular System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_Modular_System)

### Inferences
- **LM-1 early panel as nine columns (inferred).** Each column is a soft/single sound on top and its loud version or partner below. Likely columns:
  1. bass/BASS
  2. snare/SNARE
  3. hihat/HIHAT
  4. ?/HIHAT> (open)
  5. cabasa/CABASA
  6. tamb?/TAMB
  7. tom hi?/tom lo
  8. conga hi?/cong lo
  9. clave/cowbell

  Handclaps must sit in one of top-row slots 4, 6, 7 or 8. The count works: 12 voices + 5 accent buttons + open hat = 18 buttons. The later panel has a different arrangement.
- **LinnDrum top row (inferred).** Probably Hi-Hat 1, Hi-Hat 2, Hi-Hat Open, tom, Mid Tom, tom, Ride 1, Ride 2.
  - With Percussion lit, the same 8 buttons probably play Cabasa 1, Cabasa 2, Tamb 1, Tamb 2, Conga Hi, Conga Lo, Cowbell, Clap. That is 8 percussion sounds for 8 buttons; the exact order is unverified.
  - 8 top + 7 bottom = 15 pads, which matches Wikipedia's count.
- **DMX top and middle rows (inferred).** Probably Bass 1/2, Snare 1/2, closed/accented hat, Tom 1/2, Tom 4/5, ride unaccented/accented, tambourine soft/accented, shaker soft/accented. This is based on the bottom-row names plus the manual's voice descriptions. The manual says the trigger inputs act like the bottom row of buttons, so the bottom row is each voice's main sound.
- **DX numbering (inferred).** Bass 1/2/3 and Snare 1/2/3 in the MIDI chart probably equal SOFT/MEDIUM/LOUD (panel top to bottom).
- **Akai Linndrum.** Treated as LinnDrum sounds; see question 5.

- The Linn 9000 pad-by-pad order was not found.
- The SP-12 default pad/bank assignment of the 24 ROM sounds was not found. The owner's manual hosts (manualslib, manualmachine, manualshive, manuals.online) all blocked access.

## 2. Which sounds share one voice channel (and so cut each other off), and which are alternates on one channel?

### Takeaway
Documented sharing:
- **DX:** six voices of three sounds each, and only one sound per voice at a time.
- **DMX:** eight voice cards of three sounds each, with eight-voice polyphony.
- **Drumulator:** eight output channels, four of them shared by two sounds.
- **Linn 9000:** four toms on two voices; the crash and ride pairs follow the same rule.

For the LM-1 and LinnDrum only the hi-hat (open/closed on one voice) is well supported. Their other sharing is unknown.

### Cited Findings
- **DX:** "The DX contains six sets of voices. Each voice has three variations, and some of the voices contain two completely different sounds. Only one voice variation can be played at a time." — [DX manual p.10](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf). Groups:
  - BASS soft/medium/loud
  - SNARE soft/medium/loud
  - HI-HAT closed/accent/open
  - TOMS hi/mid/low
  - CYMBALS ride/ride 2/crash
  - PERC shaker 1/shaker 2/claps
- **DMX:** eight sets of voices with three variations each. CYMBAL = ride + crash, PERC 1 = tambourine + rimshot, PERC 2 = shaker + claps. — [DMX manual p.16](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf). "Maximum of 8-voice polyphony (one voice per card)." — [Wikipedia: Oberheim DMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberheim_DMX)
- **DMX tuning sharing:** each channel has one tuning control for all its sounds, so tuning the shaker also tunes the claps. All six toms use the same sample. The two tom channels are identical apart from stereo panning (one left, one right) and are usually tuned differently. — [DMX sample-disk README 1994](https://www-test.nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/dmx/00README.TXT)
- **Drumulator output jacks** (§6B):

  | Jack | Sounds on it |
  |---|---|
  | BASS | Bass drum |
  | SNARE/RIM | Snare and rim |
  | HATS | Closed and open hi hat |
  | RIDE | Ride |
  | CLAP | Handclaps |
  | CLAVE/CWB | Clave and cowbell |
  | H/M TOMS | High and mid toms |
  | LO TOM | Lo tom |
  | MIX OUT | Composite of all drums |

  Source: [Drumulator manual §6B](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- **Linn 9000:** "The four toms are different tunings of two samples, so only two can sound at once. The two crash cymbals and two rides follow the same rule." — [ES&CM May 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-beating-heart/3428). Polyphony is 13 voices for 18 sounds. — [Wikipedia: Linn 9000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_9000)
- **LM-1 hi-hat:** "the hihat proms were read constantly in a loop while only a VCA was triggered". The closed hat is produced by discharging through a path set by the decay pot, so open and closed are one voice. — [vLinn LM-1 manual](https://www.alyjameslab.com/exclusive/VLINN_Linn_LM-1Drums_USER_MANUAL_V2.pdf) (secondary, from reverse engineering). Polyphony is 10 voices (Rev 1) or 9 voices (Rev 2/3) for 12 sounds. — [Wikipedia: Linn LM-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_LM-1)
- **LinnDrum:** the sidestick follows the snare's tuning. The three toms share one sample but tune individually; the hi and lo congas share one sample and tune individually. — [LinnDrum README](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/linndrum/00README.TXT). Polyphony is 12 voices for 15 sounds. — [Wikipedia: LinnDrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinnDrum)
- **SP-12:** "8 voice polyphonic (8 outputs)". — [CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~nolting/220c)

### Inferences
- Drumulator: the shared jacks very likely mean shared playback channels, so snare/rim, open/closed hat, clave/cowbell and hi/mid tom cut each other off. The manual only describes jacks, so this is inferred.
- DMX: one card can play one sound at a time, so the choke groups are:
  - closed/accent/open hat
  - Tom 1–3
  - Tom 4–6
  - ride/crash
  - tambourine/rimshot
  - shaker/claps
  - each set of level variants

  This rests on the "one voice per card" architecture and the DX manual's explicit rule for the same design family. The DMX manual itself does not state it.
- LinnDrum: open and closed hat probably share one voice, by analogy with the LM-1 and because there is a single open-hat decay control. Snare and sidestick may share a voice, given the shared tuning. With 12 voices for 15 sounds, at least three sounds share.
- Linn 9000: the hi-hat (closed plus 7 open-decay positions) is one voice. Which two of the four tom pads pair onto one voice is unknown.
- SP-12: sounds assigned to the same output channel probably cut each other off. Not verified.
- On every machine, the level variants (soft/medium/loud, normal/accent) are the same sound on the same voice. In a Drum Rack they are best built as velocity layers or a choke group.

- Which LM-1 and LinnDrum sounds share voice hardware, beyond the hi-hat.
- The SP-12's rules for assigning sounds to channels.

## 3. What controls does each voice have, and how do accent, flam, swing and tuning work?

### Takeaway
- **Tuning:** plus or minus half an octave per voice on the DMX and DX. Every voice on the LM-1. Snare, toms and congas only on the LinnDrum. Every drum on the Linn 9000, as a static setting. None documented on the Drumulator.
- **Dynamics:** separate fixed-level buttons on the Linn, DMX and DX machines. Programmable 0–15 level plus accent on the Drumulator. Velocity pads on the Linn 9000 and SP-12.
- **Swing:** 50–71% in six steps on the DMX and Drumulator; 50–70% on the Linn 9000.

### Cited Findings

#### Linn LM-1
- Each voice has a level fader and can be panned left, centre or right. All voices are tunable (range not stated). Each voice has its own output, and using it takes the voice out of the front-panel fader's control. — [E&MM 1981](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-linn-lm-1-drum-computer/3566)
- Accent: five accent buttons (bass, snare, hi-hat, cabasa, tambourine). The reviewer finds them too loud and wants an accent-level control. — [E&MM 1981](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-linn-lm-1-drum-computer/3566)
- Auto-Correct: off, or one of 1/8 triplet, 1/16, 1/16 triplet, 1/32, 1/32 triplet. Swing is done through Auto-Correct (no percentage given). Up to 100 beats; 8 chains of up to 99 links. Clock Out runs from 1/4 to 1/192 notes. — [E&MM 1981](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-linn-lm-1-drum-computer/3566)
- Swing works by delaying alternate sixteenth notes. Each sound has level, pan and tuning. — [Wikipedia: Linn LM-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_LM-1)
- The LM-1 Auto-Correct also has a "HI" setting. Linn products run off 48 pulses per quarter note. — [Drumulator manual §6F](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- From the vLinn emulation manual (secondary): [vLinn manual](https://www.alyjameslab.com/exclusive/VLINN_Linn_LM-1Drums_USER_MANUAL_V2.pdf)
  - Hard pan switches (left/centre/right).
  - Each voice's tune knob sets the EPROM read clock; stock range is about 12,048–30,303 Hz depending on voice.
  - A hi-hat decay control sets the closed-hat decay.
  - The pads for Bass, Snare, Hihat and Tamb have two velocity values; the Tom and Conga pads trigger the high or low voice.

#### LinnDrum
- Individual level and pan for all sounds. Tuning for snare, tom and conga only. 56 user patterns, 42 preset patterns, 49 songs. DIN sync, five trigger inputs. — [Wikipedia: LinnDrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinnDrum)
- Only the open hi-hat's decay is adjustable. Each sound has its own output in addition to a stereo out. — [LinnDrum README](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/linndrum/00README.TXT)
- Dynamics are fixed-level buttons: Bass 2 levels, Snare 3, closed Hi-Hat 2, Ride 2, Cabasa 2, Tamb 2. The retrofit plays these from MIDI velocity: 1–80 / 81–127 for two-level sounds; 1–30 / 31–80 / 81–127 for Snare and Sidestick. — [Kenton LinnDrum Mk2/3](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN26000-20250619.pdf)
- The LinnDrum uses a 48-pulses-per-quarter-note clock. — [Drumulator manual §5D/6F](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)

#### Linn 9000
- Volume and pan sliders for each drum. Tuning is static, set with the volume sliders after pressing Tune Drums, at one tuning per drum per pattern. — [E&MM Apr 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/linn-9000/1874)
- Hi-hat decay is set per strike: in a second pass, via a slider, or with a footpedal. — [E&MM Apr 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/linn-9000/1874). There are 8 programmable positions, "seven open hi-hat positions in addition to the closed hi-hat". — [Wikipedia: Linn 9000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_9000)
- Velocity- and pressure-sensitive pads. With Repeat held, pressure sets the level of repeated notes. — [E&MM Apr 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/linn-9000/1874)
- Swing 0–5, equal to 50%–70%, on eighth or sixteenth correction. Auto-correct to 8th, 16th and 32nd notes plus triplets. BPM with 0.1 resolution, or tapped in. — [E&MM Apr 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/linn-9000/1874)
- Tempo range 48–250 BPM. Per-drum dynamics are stored per sequence. — [ES&CM May 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-beating-heart/3428)

#### Oberheim DMX
- Each voice can be tuned up or down half an octave with pitch controls inside the unit, on top of each voice card. Pitch can also be driven by pedals or CV. — [DMX manual p.16](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- CV in is 0 to +6 V; a higher voltage lowers the pitch; CV is not recorded. The trigger inputs act like the bottom-row buttons and fire at +1 V or more. — [DMX manual p.24](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- Faders "have no effect on the individual outputs". — [DMX manual panel diagram](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- Quantize values: 04, 06, 08, 12, 16 (default), 24, 32, 48, OFF (1/192). — [DMX manual p.8](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- Swing defaults to 50% and runs "between 50% and 71% in six steps". The manual's example steps through 50, 54, 58, 62 and 66%. Swing applies to eighths or sixteenths; any other quantize setting shows "WRONG QUANT". — [DMX manual p.9–10](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- Tempo 25–250 BPM; 100 sequences; 50 songs; 2,000 events of up to eight simultaneous notes. — [DMX manual p.32](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)
- Wikipedia mentions rolls, flams and timing variations. — [Wikipedia: Oberheim DMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberheim_DMX). I did not find these in the manual pages I read.
- There are no pan controls on the panel. The rear has LEFT, MONO and RIGHT mix outputs. — [DMX manual rear panel](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/oberheim-dmx-manual.pdf)

#### Oberheim DX
- Each voice's pitch can be tuned up or down half an octave with the rear-panel pitch controls, giving six DRUM TUNING pots. Direct outputs, one per voice, plus stereo and mono mixed outputs. The mixer faders do not affect the individual outputs. — [DX manual p.2–3, p.10](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf)
- Quantize runs from 1-2 to 1-96 plus OFF (1/192); the default is 1/16. Swing defaults to 50% (the manual's example uses 70%) and works with quantize 1-2 to 1-48. — [DX manual p.12–13](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf)
- Flams: hold RECORD while pressing the drum button, and a second note is recorded right after. This only works with quantize at 1-64, 1-96 or OFF. — [DX manual p.15](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf)
- Tempo 25–250 BPM; 100 sequences; 50 songs; 2,200 events. — [DX manual p.38](https://www.polynominal.com/site/studio/gear/drum/Oberheim_Dx/oberheim-dx-manual.pdf). A 1984 review says 30–250 BPM, and that "Snare, bass, and cymbal can be accented". — [ES&CM Jan 1984](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/oberheim-vs-drumulator/3222)

#### E-mu Drumulator
- Level: each sound has a volume from 0 (off) to 15. — [Drumulator manual §1D](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Accent:
  - Runs from 0 to 15 and is added to the level; level plus accent cannot exceed 15.
  - The accented version is assigned to its own Drum Play button.
  - The accent "floats" with any level changes.
  - Source: [Drumulator manual §1E](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Levels and accents are stored per segment and per song, and they also affect the individual outputs. — [Drumulator manual §3G–3H, §6B–6C](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Auto Correct: 8, 8/3, 16, 16/3, 32, 32/3, plus Hr (high resolution); the default is 8. — [Drumulator manual §2E, §7A](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Swing factor: 50%, 54%, 58%, 63%, 67%, 71% (buttons 1–6). — [Drumulator manual §2K](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- Tempo 40–240 BPM. 36 segments (6 banks × 6) of up to 99 measures; 8 songs of up to 99 steps; tempo changes within songs. — [Drumulator manual overview, §1F–1G](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- No tuning or decay control is described in the manual. The 1984 review does not mention tuning either. — [ES&CM Jan 1984](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/oberheim-vs-drumulator/3222)

#### E-mu SP-12
- Samples are edited with truncate and decay/detune. — [CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~nolting/220c)
- Eight sliders set sounds' pitch and volume (SP-1200 description). Swing, quantization, tempo and mix changes can be applied to patterns and songs. — [Wikipedia: E-mu SP-1200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-1200)
- Pad dynamics come from a piezo sensor. Sync to and from MIDI, SMPTE and analog click. — [Wikipedia: E-mu SP-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-12)

### Inferences
- For a faithful Drum Rack:
  - DMX/DX: put the three level variants on velocity zones of one pad, or on three pads in a choke group. Treat the stated half-octave tuning as the maximum transpose range.
  - Drumulator: levels are 16 steps, and accent adds up to 15 steps without passing 15.
  - Linn 9000: use the 50–70% swing range.

### Gaps
- LinnDrum swing range and timing-correction values.
- LinnDrum and Linn 9000 tuning ranges.
- SP-12 swing and quantize values, multi-pitch/multi-level modes, and the slider's exact parameter set.

## 4. Built-in effects or filters, and default MIDI note maps

### Takeaway
- **Effects:** none of these machines has user effects. The SP-12 has hard-wired dynamic analog low-pass filters on outputs 1–2. The LM-1 (later revisions) reportedly has fixed low-pass filtering on its bass, tom and conga voices.
- **MIDI:** only the DX, Linn 9000 and SP-12 had MIDI from the factory, and only the DX's default note map was found. LM-1 and LinnDrum maps come from Kenton retrofit kits.

### Cited Findings
- **SP-12 filters:** a "dynamic analog filter on output channels 1,2 (SSM2044 ship)", described as a "4-pole time-varying low pass filter". The page doesn't say whether channels 3–8 are filtered. — [CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~nolting/220c)
- **SP-1200 filters (successor):** channels 1 and 2 have dynamic SSM2044 filters, filtering uses "three pairs of analog lowpass filters at successively higher frequencies", and "channels 7 and 8 are unfiltered entirely". The SP-1200 adds unfiltered copies of channels 1–6 "(not available on SP-12)". — [Wikipedia: E-mu SP-1200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-1200)
- **SP-12 sample rate:** 12-bit, 27,500 Hz. — [r-massive](https://zine.r-massive.com/?p=5771); [CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~nolting/220c). Wikipedia instead gives 26.04 kHz, with no reconstruction filter. — [Wikipedia: E-mu SP-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-12). This is a conflict.
- **LM-1 voice filters:** in later units, voices with bass content (Bass, Toms, Congas) pass through a CEM3320 VCF whose CV lets transients through unfiltered. — [vLinn manual](https://www.alyjameslab.com/exclusive/VLINN_Linn_LM-1Drums_USER_MANUAL_V2.pdf) (secondary).
- **Drumulator:** uses "8-bit samples for 12 sounds, including SSM analog filters". — [Wikipedia: E-mu Drumulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_Drumulator). The manual describes no filter control and suggests outboard reverb, echo, EQ, gates and chorus. — [Drumulator manual §6D–6E](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)
- **DX default MIDI note map**, read from the keyboard chart on manual p.31:

- **LM-1 Kenton retrofit default** (not original; the LM-1 predates MIDI). Normal hits transmit velocity 64 and accents 127; on receive, velocity 1–64 plays soft and 65–127 plays loud. — [Kenton LM-1 later](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN16002-20250619.pdf), and the same map in [early](https://kentonuk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/LIN06005-20250619.pdf)

  |---|---|---|---|
  | 36 | Bass 1 & 2 | 44 | Tom Med |

- **Linn 9000:** has MIDI In/Out/Thru. At the time of the April 1985 review, "MIDI is not used for controlling drum sounds for now". No drum note map was given. — [E&MM Apr 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/linn-9000/1874)
- **DMX:** no MIDI originally. Later OS 4.01 added MIDI, and third-party interfaces exist. — [Polynominal DMX](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/index.html); [Wikipedia: Oberheim DMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberheim_DMX)

  - In RS-232 mode, the low 4 bits pick the sound: 0 Clave, 1 Cowbell, 2 Claps, 3 Open Hi Hat, 4 Closed Hi Hat, 5 Ride, 6 Bass, 7 Snare, 8 Rimshot, 9 HiTom, A MidTom, B LowTom, C Metronome.

### Inferences
- **SP-12 channels 3–6** probably have fixed low-pass filters and **7–8** are unfiltered, as on the SP-1200 (unverified for the SP-12). For a Drum Rack, a 4-pole low-pass that closes as the sound decays, on two pads only, would approximate outputs 1–2.
- **DX map vs General MIDI:** the DX map's main kit notes match the later General MIDI drum map: 36 kick, 38 snare, 39 clap, 42/44/46 hats, 43/47/50 toms, 49 crash, 51 ride.

### Gaps
- Default MIDI drum maps for the Linn 9000, SP-12 and MIDI-equipped DMX were not found.
- No primary source on the Drumulator's or LinnDrum's internal filtering.

## 5. What does each pack folder contain?

### Takeaway
Most folders combine two different sample sets:
- **Mono set:** files prefixed with the machine name (e.g. "DMXKick01", "LinnHat_P", "DrumulatorETomHi").
- **Stereo set:** generic names (e.g. "Bassdrum-01", "Tom H", "Hat Closed").

All files are 44.1 kHz 16-bit one-shots. There are no loops.

"Akai Linndrum" is Linn sounds. Its kick, clap, cowbell, tambourine and cabasa (labelled "Shuffle") match the LM-1/LinnDrum samples almost exactly, but play about 4–10% slower. Its cymbals and toms match nothing in these packs.

The LM-1 "MT-32" subfolder is unrelated Roland MT-32 material.

### Cited Findings
Source for this section: local read-only `ls -R`, `afinfo` metadata, and a local normalised cross-correlation check of the WAVs. The check used the first 0.3 s after onset and searched playback rates within ±15% for cross-folder matches and ±3% within folders. All paths are under `~/Splice/sounds/packs/`.

- **Linn LM1:** 13 mono WAVs, one per stock voice, named by sound:
  - LM1 Kick, LM1 Snare, LM1 Hat Cl, LM1 Hat Open, LM1 Cabasa, LM1 Tamb, LM1 Tom Hi, LM1 Tom Lo, LM1 Conga Hi, LM1 Conga Lo, LM1 Cowbell, LM1 Clave, LM1 Clap.
  - No accent, level or tuned variants. "LM1 Clave" lasts only 0.016 s.
- **Linn LM1/MT-32:** 30 mono WAVs plus `MT32Drums.txt`.
  - The text file says: "Roland MT-32 Drum Samples… 44.1kHz, 16bit, mono… 6 October 1998… made using an Akai S3000XL… No effects or EQ… No normalising… same velocity".
  - Sounds: agogo hi/lo, bongo hi/lo, cabasa, claves, conga hi/lo/muted hi, cowbell, crash, hand clap, closed hat, open hat 1 (3.75 s) and 2, kick, maracas, quijada, ride, rimshot, samba whistle long/short, acoustic and electronic snare, tambourine, timbale hi/lo, tom hi/mid/low.
  - These belong to a different machine and should not be mapped as LM-1 sounds.
- **Linn LinnDrum:** 52 WAVs.
  - 24 mono at the root: LinnKick01–02, LinnSnare01–09, LinnHat_C/_O/_P, LinnTom01–02, LinnRide, LinnCrash, LinnClap, LinnCowbell, LinnRimshot, LinnShaker, LinnTamborine, LinnRatchet.

    - Snaredrums: Sidestick-01..03, "Snarderum-01..03" (sic).
  - Measured near-duplicates:
    - Bassdrum ≈ LinnKick02 (0.977) ≈ LinnKick01 (0.942).
    - LinnSnare09 ≈ Snarderum-02 (0.974).
    - Hat Closed-01 ≈ -02 (0.961).
    - LinnCowbell ≈ Percussion/Cowbell (0.943).
    - LinnTom01 ≈ Tom L-01 (0.938).
    - LinnHat_C ≈ LinnHat_P (0.923).
- **Akai Linndrum:** 13 stereo WAVs: Bassdrum, SD, Closed Hat, Open Hat, Ride, Crash (2.2 s), Clap, Cowbell, Tambourin, Shuffle, Tom H, Tom M, Tom L. Best matches against all other packs (correlation, rate factor):

  - SD ↔ LinnSnare03: 0.888.
  - Closed Hat ↔ Linn 9000 Hat Closed: 0.79 (weak).
  - Crash, Ride, Open Hat: no match (≤0.18). Toms: no match (≤0.70).
- **Linn 9000:** 34 WAVs.
  - 14 mono: "Linn 9000 bell, cabasa, clap, conga, cowbell, crash, hat cl, hat open, kick, ride, snare, stick, tamb, tom".
  - 20 stereo: BAssdrum, Conga H/M/L, Cowbell-01/02, Crash-01..03, Hat Closed, Hat Open, Ping, Rim Shot, Snaredrum, Tambourine, Tom-01..05.
  - Only one cross-set duplicate: "Conga L" = "Linn 9000 conga" (0.999).
- **Oberheim DMX:** 38 WAVs.
  - 18 mono "DMX…" files: Kick01–02, Snare01–03, Hat_C, Hat_O, TomHi/Mid/Lo, TimbaleHi/Lo, Ride, Crash, Clap, Rim, Tamborine, Cabasa.
  - 20 stereo files. Every mono file has an identical stereo twin (correlation 1.000), so the stereo set is a copy plus "Bassdrum-03" and "Timbale M".

- **Oberheim DX:** 13 mono files: DXKick, DXSnare, DXHat_C, DXHat_O, DXHat_P, DXTomHi, DXTomLo, DXTom_E, DXRide, DXCrash, DXShaker, DXClap, DXRimshot.

  - DXHat_P ≈ DXHat_C (0.848).
- **Emu Drumulator:** 39 WAVs.
  - 27 mono "Drumulator…" files: AgogoHi/Lo, Block, Cabasa, Clap, Clave, Conga01/02, Crash, ETomHi/Mid/Lo, Hat_C/_O/_P, Kick01/02, Ride, Snare01–04, Tamborine, TimbHi/Lo, Tom02, Tom03.
  - 12 stereo files: Bassdrum, Snaredrum, Rim Shot, Tom H/M/L, Claves, Cowbell, Clap, Hat Closed, Hat Open, Cymbal.

- **Emu SP12:** 68 stereo WAVs, numbered not named:
  - BASSDRUM: Bassdrum-01..14.
  - SNARES: Snaredrum-01..21, Rim Shot-01..02.
  - Toms: Tom H-01..06, Tom L-01..06, Tom M-01, 02, 03, 05 (no M-04).
  - Cymbals: Crash, Hat Closed-01/02, Hhopen1, Ride.
  - Percussion: Blow1, Clap, Cowbell.

- **Emu Modular:** 5 stereo WAVs: Bassdrum-01 (0.90 s), Bassdrum-02 (0.74 s), EmuPong (0.15 s), Flick01 (0.12 s), Flick-02 (0.055 s).

### Inferences
- **Akai Linndrum** is Linn LM-1/LinnDrum samples passed through another sampler; the folder name points to an Akai.
  - Every confident match plays at a near-constant 0.90–0.96 of the other packs' speed. The ×0.90 group is about 1.8 semitones lower, and ~0.907 is the 40 kHz/44.1 kHz ratio. That points to a sample-rate change during transfer, not separate recordings.
  - Its cymbals, open hat and toms are from an unknown source, possibly LinnDrum at other tunings.
  - It fits best under the LinnDrum: it has crash and ride, which the LM-1 lacks.
- **LM-1 and LinnDrum overlap.** In these packs, several LM-1 and LinnDrum "Percussion" files (clap, cowbell, cabasa, tambourine, and LM1 Kick vs Bassdrums/Bassdrum) match the same Akai file at the same rate. So they are probably the same recordings. Whether the LinnDrum reused LM-1 samples, or the pack mislabels them, is unknown.
- **Non-stock sounds** probably came from optional sound chips or later sampling. Unverified.
  - DMX timbales and "Cabasa" (the stock DMX has a shaker).
  - Drumulator agogo, block, cabasa, congas, crash, e-toms and timbales. Wikipedia mentions third-party Digidrums EPROMs.
  - DX "Tom_E" (the DX library lists an "Electronic Tom" chip) and "Rimshot" (library chip "Tambourine/Rimshot").
  - Linn "Ratchet", "bell" and "Ping".
  - The SP12 folder's 68 sounds are far more than the 24 ROM sounds, so most are user samples.
- **"_P" hi-hat files** (LinnHat_P, DrumulatorHat_P, DXHat_P) don't match any documented pedal-hat voice. DXHat_P may be the DX's accented hat.
- **Missing panel sounds:**
  - LM1 has none missing.
  - LinnDrum lacks a separate sidestick/snare level set, but its snare variants may cover it.
  - DMX lacks the accented hat.
  - DX lacks the mid tom (a tuning of the same tom).
  - Drumulator has all 12 stock sounds in the stereo set; "Cymbal" is the ride.
  - Linn 9000 has all of its stock sounds.

### Gaps
- Which commercial sample collections the mono and stereo sets come from, and whether they were recorded at stock tunings.

## 6. Factory or demo patterns

### Takeaway
Only the DMX documents named factory content: 78 sequences and 19 songs on a cassette. No source shows their step grids. The LinnDrum had 42 preset patterns, but no names were found. The Drumulator shipped with some factory segments, unnamed. No presets are described for the LM-1, DX or Linn 9000.

  - Seq 00 is "Demonstration". Seq 77 "End" is a single-beat ending.
  - Style groups, each with A, A Fill, B, B Fill: Rock 1A–5B (01–20), Shuffle (21–24), Honky-Tonk (25–28), Boogie (29–32), Swing (33–36), Country (37–40), Disco (41–44), Funk (45–48), Polka (49–52), Samba (53–56), Bossa (57–60), Mambo (61–64), Waltz (65–68), Reggae (69–72), 7/4 Rock (73–76).

  - The factory sequences "consume about 5/4 of the DMX's memory capacity" (sic).
- **LinnDrum:** "42 preset drum patterns", plus 56 user patterns and 49 songs. — [Wikipedia: LinnDrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinnDrum)
- **Drumulator:** "Several segments were loaded into the Drumulator at the factory". The song example uses segments 61–65, described as "already programmed into the Drumulator". — [Drumulator manual §1F, §3C](https://www.polynominal.com/emu-drumulator/emu-drumulator-manual.pdf)

### Inferences
- The DMX factory beats were on cassette data, not printed. Grids would need a recording of the factory tape or a transcription, and none was found.

### Gaps
- LinnDrum preset names and grids.
- Drumulator factory segment names.
- Any DX, SP-12 or Linn 9000 demo content.

## 7. Famous recordings that verifiably used each machine, with published step charts

### Takeaway
I found recording-level evidence for:
- **DMX:** "Blue Monday" and "Every Breath You Take" (kick only).
- **LinnDrum:** "Relax", and less securely "Firework".
- **LM-1:** Leon Russell's *Life and Love*.
- **Drumulator:** Cocteau Twins' *Treasure*.
- **SP-12:** Beastie Boys' "Rhymin & Stealin".

The Linn 9000 and other Drumulator credits are uncited claims on Wikipedia. No published step chart was found for any of these grooves. The search budget ran out before MusicRadar, Sound On Sound or Attack could be searched properly. Every grid is therefore null; none was reconstructed.

### Cited Findings
- **DMX, New Order "Blue Monday" (1983):** "An Oberheim DMX drum machine was used for rhythm" (citing Flint, *Sound on Sound*, April 2004). Gilbert wrote the sequence by hand and accidentally added a rest, which the band kept. — [Wikipedia: Blue Monday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Monday_(New_Order_song))
- **DMX, The Police "Every Breath You Take" (1983):** the DMX was used for the kick drum only (citing Buskin, *Sound on Sound*, March 2004). — [Wikipedia: Oberheim DMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberheim_DMX)
- **DMX artist links:** Polynominal cites DMX music from "New Order to Run DMC" without naming tracks. — [Polynominal DMX](https://www.polynominal.com/oberheim-dmx/index.html)
