# Simmons SDS-400, SDS-V/SDS5, AJK Percussion Synth, Visco SpaceDrum, X Drum LM8953, MXR 185, Sakata DPM-48: hardware setup, patterns, recordings, pack contents

Scope note: "Simmons SDS-5" and "Simmons SDS-V" are the same product (V is the Roman numeral; magazines of the time write "SDS 5" or "SDS5"). They are treated as one machine covering three pack folders: "Simmons SDS-5", "Simmons SDS5", "Simmons SDSV". The JSON therefore has 7 objects, not 8. — [Music UK review, Sep 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013); [KB6 archive lists "Simmons SDS-5 (SDSV)"](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php); [Wikipedia, "Simmons SDS-V"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V)

Research limits: the web-search budget (shared with other agents in this run) ran out partway through, so later work used direct page fetches only. Three primary documents could not be read: the SDS-V service manual (synth-diy.org blocks automated readers), the GearSpace Visco thread (HTTP 403), and the archived audiopervert.com site (archive.org not reachable). Claims taken from search-result excerpts of those pages are marked as such.

## Q1. What is each machine, and what voices/channels does it have, in front-panel order, with exact labels?

### Takeaway
The MXR 185 panel is fully documented: 12 buttons in a 4-column by 3-row grid. The SDS-V, SDS 400 and Sakata DPM-48 have documented voice lists, but their exact printed labels and left-to-right order are not confirmed. "AJK Percussion Synth", "X Drum LM8953" and the Visco SpaceDrum could not be identified as hardware: no maker, manual, review or photo was found. Their voice lists can only be read from the pack file names.

### Cited Findings

#### Simmons SDS-V (SDS5)
- Shown at the 1980 "Over The Road Show". The first 15 units were built by Musicaid before it folded, then Simmons built them. Discontinued at its peak around the end of 1983. — [One Two Testing, "Company Report – Simmons", Jun 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/company-report-simmons/11795)
- Released commercially in the first half of 1981 (Wikipedia, citing Matt Dean, *The Drum: A History*). Designers were Dave Simmons and Richard James Burgess; the designer credit has no citation on Wikipedia. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V). Burgess (Landscape's drummer) "worked with Simmons on the design". — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/sds5/index.html). Sequencer.de gives "built 1982" instead. — [sequencer.de](https://sequencer.de/syns/simmons/SDS5_SdsV.html)
- Early review by Seb Shelton (dated January 1981, reprinted by the Simmons Museum): six modules (hi-hat, snare, three rack toms, bass drum), RRP £1,250. Five 11-inch hexagonal pads plus a 22-inch hexagonal bass pad. The hi-hat pad comes with a pedal "that works like a volume pedal". — [Simmons Museum blog](https://blog.simmonsmuseum.com/?p=970)
- Music UK (Rick Palmer, Sep 1983): the rack holds up to seven drum-sound modules plus an output mixer. The basic kit has the mixer plus bass, snare and three tom modules. Hi-hat and cymbal modules are optional, sold separately, and use digitally sampled sound run through analogue filters. RRP £1,530.65. The review does not give the left-to-right rack order. — [Music UK](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013)
- Hollow Sun: the standard SDSV has five modules (kick, snare, three toms). Hi-hat and cymbal modules came later and were "unconvincing". Each module is a variable triangle oscillator plus a noise generator; there are no samples in the drum modules. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/sds5/index.html)
- Wikipedia lists the standard kit as bass drum, snare and three tom toms, with optional cymbal and hi-hat modules. The hi-hat has open and closed settings controlled by an external pedal. This part is uncited. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V)

#### Simmons SDS 400
- An "analog electronic drum unit with 4 different tom sound sources". The four toms are the same except that tom 1 also has a "run generator". — [Hallvard Tangeraas sample-disk README, 1994](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/sds_400/00README.TXT)
- Price £549.99. It has four toms plus a run generator, and more outputs than the two-tom SDS 200 (£359.99). Its voice circuitry is the same as the SDS 800's (the 800 has bass drum, snare and two toms). — [Electronic Soundmaker & Computer Music, Tony Reed, Sep 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/this-years-modules/3913)
- "The 400 has just four toms, also with a single stand" and a run generator. — [International Musician & Recording World, Bob Henrit, Sep 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds800-series/12653)
- Channel labels as described: Tom 1 (with run generator), Tom 2, Tom 3, Tom 4. Printed legends are not confirmed. — sources above

#### MXR 185 Drum Computer
- 12 voices on three rows of four buttons. Top row: Tom 1, Tom 2, Crash, Claps. Middle row: Hi-hat Open, Snare, Tom 3, Block. Bottom row: Hi-hat Closed, Kick (bass drum), Rim Shot, Bell. — [Electronics & Music Maker, Ian Gilby, Oct 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-185-drum-computer/6203)
- A second review describes the same grid column by column: "a 4 x 3 array with snare and bass central". Column 1: tom-tom 1, open hi-hat, closed hi-hat. Column 2: tom-tom 2, snare, bass. Column 3: crash cymbal, tom-tom 3, rim shot. Column 4: claps, wood block, cowbell. This matches E&MM row for row. — [Electronic Soundmaker & Computer Music, Webb & Jenkins, Sep 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-drum-computer/3140)
- A third review lists the voices as bass drum, snare, rim-shot snare, high, mid and low toms, open hi-hat, closed hi-hat, crash cymbal, claps, "block" and "bell". The quotation marks around "block" and "bell" suggest these are the panel words. Price £1,500. — [Music UK, Rick Palmer, Aug 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-drum-computer/12004)
- The pads are laid out loosely like a small kit, with the snare above the kick. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/mxr185/index.html)

#### Sakata DPM-48
- A digital drum machine, first released late 1983 and discontinued around 1987. Also sold as Nihon-Hammond, Jugg Box and Multivox. An 86-page English/German manual exists (sold on Reverb). — [MATRIXSYNTH, Jugg Box DPM-48 manual listing](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2025/08/jugg-box-dpm-48-1983-manual.html?m=0)
- Dated 1984 and licensed to Hammond as the DPM48. It has 22 sampled sounds; the UK price was £699, about half a Drumulator. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/sakata/index.html). Sequencer.de also says 1984, with 22 pads and "8 voices". It describes 8 volume faders for "three sound groups (2x8 and 1x6)" matching the pad layout, which reads as pad rows of 8, 8 and 6. — [sequencer.de](https://sequencer.de/syns/sakata/DPM48.html)
- The only complete sound list found comes from the 1990s sample set: accenthh, agogo1, agogo2, bass1, bass2, bass3, cabasa1, cabasa2, clap, closedhh, crash, openhh, ride, rim, snare1, snare2, tom1 to tom4. The original sampler recorded 22 sounds, but 2 were "identical (only with an added 'accent' setting)" and were dropped. — [Tangeraas / Kyle Farrell text file](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/sakata.txt)
- A seller's listing uses the panel-style names "TOMS 1-4" and "BASS 1/2/3". — [MATRIXSYNTH, Aug 2024](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2024/08/sakata-dpm-48-aka-multivox-demos.html?m=0)
- 22 samples stored as 8-bit unsigned PCM on EPROMs; made in Japan. — [MATRIXSYNTH, Apr 2025 listing](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2025/04/sakata-dpm-48-digital-drum-machine-w.html)

#### Visco Space Drum
- The KB6 sample archive lists "Visco Space Drum" (#265): "Drumset - 40 Drum Samples - 783 KB". — [KB6 downloads](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php)
- From search-result excerpts of the GearSpace thread (the page itself returned HTTP 403): users say there is "not much info on the web, can't even find a picture". The Coron DS-8 was suggested as the source and rejected as "100% not". A Univox-labelled unit was suggested but doubted ("doesn't look programmable"). The set is said to be in the KB6 archive (40 sounds) and in the "200 Drum Machines" collection (only 15 sounds), and KB6's labelling is said to be unreliable. One user guessed the name may be a play on "Disco Space Drums". — [GearSpace, "Visco Space Drum"](https://gearspace.com/threads/visco-space-drum.390269/)

#### X Drum LM8953
- No machine, chip or maker called "X Drum" or "LM8953" was found. Searches for "LM8953" returned only the unrelated Behringer LM Drum. The KB6 archive has no such entry; its nearest name is the different Pearl Drum-X. — [KB6 downloads](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php)
- The "200 Drum Machines" page on hexawe.net now shows only one file, drums.zip (301 MB, 2008-10-23), with no contents listing. — [hexawe.net](http://www.hexawe.net/mess/200.Drum.Machines/)

#### AJK Percussion Synth
- No maker or model called "AJK" was found in several searches. The KB6 archive has no AJK entry; its A-list runs Ace Tone, AcidLab, Akai, Alesis, ARP, Atari, Atlantex, Austin. — [KB6 downloads](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php)

### Inferences
- SDS-V Drum Rack order: use the order every review uses, BD, SD, T1, T2, T3, then the optional HH and CY. The physical rack order is unverified.
- MXR 185: copy the 4×3 grid exactly. In a 4×4 Drum Rack, put row 3 (HH Closed, Bass, Rim Shot, Bell) on the bottom pad row, row 2 above it, row 1 above that. This keeps the hardware's snare-above-kick geometry.
- Sakata: there are 22 pads but only 20 distinct sounds are documented. The 2 missing pads are probably accent variants (inferred from Farrell's note).
- The Visco, X Drum and AJK names most likely come from free sample collections (KB6 or "200 Drum Machines"), not from documented hardware. "Visco" could be a mislabel. Inferred, not verified.

### Gaps
- Exact printed legends and the left-to-right rack order for the SDS-V. The service manual (synth-diy.org) blocks automated readers, and no photo-captioned source was read.
- Exact printed legends on the SDS 400 panel.
- Sakata DPM-48 pad layout and the 8 voice groups. The 86-page manual exists but is not online in readable form.
- Maker, year and type of the Visco Space Drum, X Drum LM8953 and AJK Percussion Synth: no documentation found.

## Q2. Which sounds share a channel or cut each other off?

### Takeaway
On the SDS-V and SDS 400, every channel is one analog voice, so all samples from the same module are alternative settings of one channel. On the SDS-V, the hi-hat (closed, open, pedal) is one module, and rimshots come from the snare channel. On the MXR 185, the open and closed hats share one output and one slider. On the Sakata, 22 sounds feed 8 voices, so groups share a slider and an output (bass drums, snares, agogo/cabasa documented). Whether same-group sounds cut each other off is not stated in any source found.

### Cited Findings
- SDS-V: the rack has one hi-hat pedal input. — [Music UK 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013). The hi-hat has open/closed settings set by an external pedal. — [Wikipedia (uncited)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V). The pads have a metal rim that "produces rimshot-style tones when struck". — [Shelton review via Simmons Museum](https://blog.simmonsmuseum.com/?p=970)
- SDS 400: individual outputs for each of the four toms. No channel is shared. — [Tangeraas README](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/sds_400/00README.TXT)
- MXR 185: "Open and closed hi-hats have separate programming but share one output socket and one level slider." — [E&MM Oct 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-185-drum-computer/6203). They are "premixed into one output, so they share a single volume control". — [ES&CM Sep 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-drum-computer/3140). The 185 uses "a multiplexing system similar to the LinnDrum's". — [HKA Design](https://hkadesign.org.uk/mxr185midi.html)
- Sakata: individual outs exist "in groups anyway, common bass out, common snare out etc". — [Kyle Farrell text](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/sakata.txt). "Some percussion sounds, including agogo and cabasa, share one level slider." — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/sakata/index.html). There are 8 voice sliders, one per voice. — [MATRIXSYNTH 2025 listing](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2025/04/sakata-dpm-48-digital-drum-machine-w.html); [sequencer.de](https://sequencer.de/syns/sakata/DPM48.html)

### Inferences
- SDS-V and SDS 400: each analog module retriggers when hit again, so samples from one module never sounded together. In a Drum Rack, put same-channel alternatives (e.g., Hat Closed/Open/Pedal; Snaredrum + Rimshot) in one choke group. Better still, put them in one pad's chain list, switched like the SDS-V's four memory buttons with a Chain Selector.
- MXR 185: choke HH Open with HH Closed. That is how most multiplexed machines of the time worked, but the reviews only confirm the shared output and slider.
- Sakata: 8 voices for 22 sounds suggests each slider group plays one sound at a time, so the bass drums, the snares, and the agogos/cabasas would cut each other. The hi-hats probably do too. Inferred only.
- Visco, X Drum, AJK: unknown. If you want normal behaviour, choke the hats, but nothing documents it.

### Gaps
- No source says outright that MXR closed hat chokes open hat, or which Sakata sounds sit in each of the 8 groups.

## Q3. What controls does each voice have? How do accent and dynamics work? (plus global features and preset structure)

### Takeaway
SDS-V modules have six sound controls (noise, tone, bend, decay, noise/tone balance, click/drum balance) and 4 memories per module (1 fixed factory preset plus 3 user). Dynamics come from how hard the pad is hit. The SDS 400 has ten controls per tom, a run generator on Tom 1, and no memory. The MXR 185 has level sliders, one global pitch, and a per-voice, per-step accent at a fixed level. The Sakata has 8 group sliders, accented sound variants and no velocity.

### Cited Findings

#### SDS-V
- The six controls are Noise (white noise for the drum-head "flap"), Tone, Bend, Decay, Noise/Tone balance, and Click/Drum (attack click). — [Music UK 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013). The 1981 review names them "hiss pitch", "tone pitch", "bend", "decay", "balance", "click balance". — [Simmons Museum](https://blog.simmonsmuseum.com/?p=970). The service notes (seen only in a search excerpt) map factory-preset trimmers to noise pitch (R18), tone pitch (R17), bend (R1), decay (R5), click balance (R13) and noise-tone balance (R9). — [SDS-V service manual PDF](https://synth-diy.org/yg-archives2/raw/Simmons_Drums/files/16_Manuals/4_SDS-V_SM.pdf)
- Conflicting reading: Hollow Sun and Wikipedia describe the knobs as noise level, tone level, bend, decay, "noise tone" (a simple static filter) and "click drum". — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/sds5/index.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V). The service notes and the 1981 and 1983 reviews support "pitch" for Noise and Tone.
- Memories: each module stores 4 sounds. One is a non-adjustable factory preset. Two user sounds are set on rows of screwdriver pots, and one on "mixer-type" knobs. — [Music UK](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013). Selection is by "a square of four push-buttons"; top-left recalls the factory preset. — [Simmons Museum](https://blog.simmonsmuseum.com/?p=970)
- Mixer: sensitivity and output level per channel. Rear panel (all XLR): 7 pad inputs, hi-hat pedal input, individual outs, pre-panned stereo pair, mono mix, and a trigger input per channel. — [Music UK](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013)
- Dynamics: the pads are pressure-sensitive, "though dynamic response shrinks as sensitivity rises". — [Music UK](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013). Trigger strength affects the sound. — [One Two Testing 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/company-report-simmons/11795). The click control adds attack based on pad velocity, and triggering from drum machines sounded weaker because much of the presence comes from the click. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/sds5/index.html)
- It can be triggered from a TR-808 or an MC-4 sequencer. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/sds5/index.html)

#### SDS 400
- Controls per drum: trigger sensitivity, filter pitch, filter sweep, 2nd skin level, tone pitch, bend, decay, balance (tone/white noise), click level, output level. — [Tangeraas README](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/sds_400/00README.TXT)
- Shared 800-series panel order: Sensitivity (with tell-tale light), Filter Pitch, Filter Sweep, Click Pitch (bass drum only), Tone Pitch, Bend, Decay, Noise to Tone, Click Level, Instrument Level. Toms have Second Skin, which adds harmonics by modulating the source frequency. — [IM&RW Sep 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds800-series/12653)
- Run generator on Tom 1 only: Run Threshold, Step Time, Run Time, Balance, Up/Down, On/Off. A footswitch is supplied, with a rear jack for remote on/off. Second Skin ranges from a subtle double-headed tom to "metallic clangs and ring-modulation". Outputs: individual, Mix, Stereo. A 5-pin DIN socket takes the SDS6, the Simmons CBM64 sequencer or MPC programmers. — [ES&CM Sep 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/this-years-modules/3913)
- The run generator "varies the pitch of this drum either sequentially or randomly". — [Tangeraas README](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/sds_400/00README.TXT)

#### MXR 185
- Eleven level sliders (the hats share one) and individual ¼" outputs. Pitch is a single global control that is not stored in memory. "An Accent button emphasises individual beats and works separately on each voice", but "the accent level is not adjustable; it is preset internally for each voice". — [E&MM Oct 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-185-drum-computer/6203)
- 14 sliders in total: 11 voice levels plus tempo, pitch and click-track volume. — [ES&CM Sep 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-drum-computer/3140)
- Individual sounds cannot be tuned; all pitches move together. The fetched summary gives the range as ±1%, which looks small and is worth checking against the original page. — [Music UK Aug 1983](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-drum-computer/12004)
- The pads have no velocity sensitivity; dynamics come from the ACCENT button. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/mxr185/index.html). Accent can also come through the pedal input, which also handles start. — [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/sample-packs/mxr-185/). The MIDI retrofit maps note velocity to accent. — [HKA Design](https://hkadesign.org.uk/mxr185midi.html)
- Global features: Shift 0–3 "randomly retard or advance beats", with level 3 giving a jazz-type swing. Accuracy has seven levels from ¼-note triplets to 1/32-note triplets. Tempo 40–250 BPM. Patterns 00–99, up to 99 beats each, and up to 100 songs. Tape click at 24 pulses per quarter note, From Tape external drive, 15-pin Trigger Inputs (5 V TTL), External Voice connector. Z80 CPU, 8-bit DAC0800, 52K PROM of which the crash takes 24K. — [E&MM](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-185-drum-computer/6203). Polynominal says 99 patterns and 99 songs. — [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/sample-packs/mxr-185/)

#### Sakata DPM-48
- Each sound has its own volume pot (user review). Tempo is set with two wheels. A 2-digit display. Pads not velocity-sensitive. Outputs: 2 stereo mains, headphone, 8 individual outs. Real-time layer recording or grid step entry. — [Audiofanzine user reviews](https://en.audiofanzine.com/drum-machine/sakata/dpm-48/)
- 8 voice sliders plus a general volume knob. Stereo output with sounds spread across the field. — [MATRIXSYNTH 2025](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2025/04/sakata-dpm-48-digital-drum-machine-w.html)
- On/off pads with "accented" variations; separate metronome output; DIN sync to TR-808 or MC-4B. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/sakata/index.html)
- 48 patterns of up to 32 steps plus 3 songs. Roland DIN sync and trigger inputs. — [Kyle Farrell text](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/sakata.txt). Patterns live on a RAM cartridge; mid-1984 alternative sound sets for the DPM-48E were "Electronic Drums", Rock and Latin Percussion. — [MATRIXSYNTH manual listing](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2025/08/jugg-box-dpm-48-1983-manual.html?m=0)
- Without the RAM cartridge it cannot be programmed or put into run mode. — [MATRIXSYNTH Aug 2024](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2024/08/sakata-dpm-48-aka-multivox-demos.html?m=0)

#### Visco, X Drum, AJK
- No controls documented. See Q1.

### Inferences
- SDS-V in Ableton: one pad per module. Each pad holds up to 4 alternative samples in a Chain Selector, mirroring the 4 memory buttons. Velocity should drive both level and attack brightness, since pad force drove the click. Keep velocity range compressed if mimicking high Sensitivity settings.
- SDS 400: the 3 "Slap" samples are noise-heavy balance settings of the same tom circuit, not a separate channel. A random or stepping pitch change on Tom 1 (e.g., a Random MIDI effect on pitch) would imitate the run generator.
- MXR 185: give each pad two velocity levels, normal and accent, with a fixed per-voice accent gain. Map one macro to transpose on every pad to copy the single global pitch.
- Sakata: there are no velocity layers; the accent is a different sound, so map accent samples to their own pads.

### Gaps
- Exact accent gain per voice (MXR, Sakata), and how accents are entered on the Sakata.
- Control legends for the SDS-V hi-hat and cymbal modules.
- SDS 400 launch date (1985 is inferred from the Sept 1985 reviews).

## Q4. Any built-in effects?

### Takeaway
None of the documented machines has built-in effects. For Visco, X Drum and AJK, this is unknown.

### Cited Findings
- SDS-V: the reviews list voice controls, mixer, outputs and pads, and no effects. The 1981 review mentions sending the rack to a mixing desk for effects. — [Music UK](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013); [Simmons Museum](https://blog.simmonsmuseum.com/?p=970)
- SDS 400: no effects listed; Second Skin and the filter sweep are part of the voice. — [ES&CM](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/this-years-modules/3913)
- MXR 185: no effects in three full reviews. — [E&MM](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-185-drum-computer/6203)
- Sakata DPM-48: no effects in any source found. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/sakata/index.html)

### Inferences
- "None" for SDS-V, SDS 400, MXR 185 and Sakata is based on reviews that list every feature without mentioning effects; no source says "no effects" in so many words.

### Gaps
- Visco, X Drum, AJK: unknown.

## Q5. What does each pack folder contain?

### Takeaway
All folders hold 16-bit/44.1 kHz one-shot WAVs and no loops. Two folders can be traced to a documented source: SDS-400 matches a 1994 sample disk exactly, and MXR 185 matches the KB6 set byte for byte. The Sakata folder matches the 1990s Farrell/Tangeraas set. The three SDS-V folders do not say which preset or memory each hit came from.

### Cited Findings
(Contents come from a read-only `ls -R` and file metadata on this Mac, 2026-11-13.)
- **Simmons SDS-400**: 20 stereo files, Slap-1..3 and Tom-01..17, flat folder, 0.23–1.16 s. The 1994 disk holds "3 'white noise' type 'slap' sounds" and "17 electronic tom (snare?) sounds" named SLAP_1..3 and TOM_01..17. It matches exactly. — [Tangeraas README and file list](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/sds_400/00README.TXT)
- **Simmons SDS-5**: 64 stereo files in four folders. BASSDRUM has Bassdrum-01..12. SNARES has Snaredrum-01..21 and Rimshot-01..07. Toms has Tom-01..17. CYMBALS has Hat Closed-01..03, Hat Open-01..02 and Hat Pedal-01..02. Numbered with no gaps; despite the folder name there is no crash or ride. The KB6 SDS-5 set has 109 samples, so this is not it. — [KB6](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php)
- **Simmons SDS5**: 23 mono files with short names: SDKick01–04, SDSnare01–07, SDPop01–02, SDTomLo/Med/Mid/Hard/Noise/EHi/ELo, SDSTomE2 (naming outlier) and SDHat_C/SDHat_O. 0.11–0.55 s. Source unknown.
- **Simmons SDSV**: 45 mono files. sds5_kits holds 13 named hits: kick 1–3, "Kick 4", "Snare hi 1", "snare hi 2", snare lo, snare mid, Crack, tom 1–4. Capitalisation is inconsistent, and 4 files are exactly 0.186 s long, which suggests truncation. sds5_misc holds "SDS5 01"–"SDS5 32", numbered only with no voice names, 0.32–0.81 s. Source unknown.
- **AJK Percussion Synth**: 5 stereo files: Bassdrum, Cowbell, Noise, Snarepop, Tom. All 0.05–0.25 s. Source unknown.
- **Visco SpaceDrum**: 40 stereo files in BASSDRUM (11), CYMBALS (Hat Closed-01..04, Hat Closed Reversed, Hat Open-01..03, Hat Pedal-01), MISC (Bleep-01..02, Cowbell, Synth Tom H, Synth Tom L, Synth Tom M-01..02, Woodblock1, Woodblock2), SNARES (Rimshot, Snaredrum-01..03) and Toms (Tom-01..06, "Tom7"). The 40-sample count matches KB6 #265. — [KB6](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php)
- **X Drum LM8953**: 22 stereo files, flat: Bassdrum-01..02, Crash, Hat Closed, Hat Open, Hat Pedal, Ride, Rim Shot-01..02, Snare Fill (1.17 s), Snaredrum-01..04, Tambourine, Tom-01..06, phil-drm. Many files have identical lengths: 0.4644 s for five files, 1.0107 s for two. Source unknown.
- **MXR 185**: 12 mono files in subfolder "mxr185": MXR Claps, Cowbell, Crash, Hat_closed, Hat_open, Kick, Rimshot, Snare, Tom 1–3, woodblock. One file per hardware voice, complete. The total of 284,652 bytes matches KB6's "MXR 185, 12 Drum Samples, 284 KB". — [KB6](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php)
- **Sakata DPM-48**: 20 stereo files: Agogo1, Agogo2, Bassdrum-01..03, Cabasa-01..02, Clap, Crash, Hat Closed-01..02, Hat Open, Ride, Rim, Snaredrum-01..02, Tom-01..04. These are the same 20 sounds as the Farrell/Tangeraas set, which dropped 2 accent duplicates. KB6's set has 23 samples, so this folder is not it. — [sakata.txt](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/sci/audio/instruments/yamaha/DRUMS/sakata.txt); [KB6](https://samples.kb6.de/downloads.php)

### Inferences
- In the Sakata folder, one of the two "Hat Closed" files is very likely the "accent HH". Hat Closed-01 is about 1.4× the length of -02, which matches the accenthh/closedhh size ratio in the 1990s set.
- "Hat Closed Reversed" (Visco) is an edit, not a hardware voice.
- "Snare Fill" (X Drum) is probably a recorded multi-hit phrase.
- In the SDS-V folders, "Rimshot" and "Crack" files belong to the snare channel, and "Pop" files are probably snare or tom presets.
- The SDS-V packs give many alternatives per channel (e.g., 12 kicks) and the SDS 400 gives 17 toms, so a faithful rack chooses up to 4 per channel. That mirrors the SDS-V's 4 memories; the SDS 400 had no memories, only knobs.

### Gaps
- The source collections for the SDS-5, SDS5, SDSV, AJK and X Drum folders are not confirmed. The "200 Drum Machines" zip could not be inspected without downloading it.

## Q6. Factory patterns (names and step grids)

### Takeaway
None of these machines has documented factory patterns. The SDS-V and SDS 400 are pad-played sound modules with no sequencer. No step grids were copied, because no source shows any.

### Cited Findings
- SDS-V: no sequencer; the SDS6 sequencer was sold separately and drives the per-channel trigger inputs. — [Music UK](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/simmons-sds-5-electronic-drums-kit-and-sds-6-sequencer/12013)
- SDS 400: sequencing only from an external SDS6 or CBM64 via DIN. — [ES&CM](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/this-years-modules/3913)
- MXR 185: three 1983 reviews describe 100 user patterns and songs and mention no preset or demo patterns. — [E&MM](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-185-drum-computer/6203); [ES&CM](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-drum-computer/3140); [Music UK](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/mxr-drum-computer/12004)
- Sakata: the 48 patterns sit on a RAM cartridge, and the machine will not run without it. — [MATRIXSYNTH Aug 2024](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2024/08/sakata-dpm-48-aka-multivox-demos.html?m=0)

### Inferences
- The MXR 185 and Sakata most likely shipped with empty user memory. For the Sakata, patterns needing the RAM cartridge suggests there were no ROM presets. Not confirmed.

### Gaps
- The Sakata manual (86 pp.) could not be read; it may include example patterns. Visco, X Drum and AJK are unknown.

## Q7. Famous recordings that used each machine, with published step charts

### Takeaway
Only the SDS-V has reasonably evidenced recordings: Thomas Dolby's "She Blinded Me with Science" (Simmons drums, model inferred), plus weaker claims for Spandau Ballet and Landscape. No verified recordings were found for the SDS 400, MXR 185, Sakata, Visco, X Drum or AJK. No published step chart was found for any of the grooves, so all grid fields are null.

### Cited Findings
- **Thomas Dolby, "She Blinded Me with Science" (1982)**: kick and snare are Simmons, "the tom-toms are from the Simmons module fed through the bass amp", triggered from Dolby's PPG drum unit. The Simmons model is not named. — [Mix, "Classic Tracks", Tim Morse](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks-thomas-dolbys-she-blinded-me-science-365232)
- **Spandau Ballet**: drummer John Keeble bought one of the first SDS5s. — [One Two Testing 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/company-report-simmons/11795). Wikipedia credits "Chant No. 1 (I Don't Need This Pressure On)" (1981, produced by Burgess) without a citation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V)
- **Landscape**: Wikipedia says, without a citation, that Burgess triggered a prototype from a Roland MC-8 for *From the Tea-Rooms of Mars... to the Hell-Holes of Uranus*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V)
- "In one week seven of the top ten singles had SDS5 on them" (the singles are not named). — [One Two Testing 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/company-report-simmons/11795)
- OMD's Malcolm Holmes used Simmons toms with a real kick, snare and hi-hat on the 1983 *Dazzle Ships* tour (live use, not tied to a recording). — [Electricity Club interview](https://www.electricityclub.co.uk/mal-holmes/)
- Other SDS-V credits on Wikipedia have no citations and were not verified: Elton John *Ice on Fire* and *Leather Jackets* (Gus Dudgeon), Jean-Michel Jarre *Concerts in China*, the *Dempsey and Makepeace* theme, *The Equalizer* theme (Stewart Copeland). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_SDS-V)
- MXR 185: Hollow Sun, Polynominal and the three 1983 reviews name no artists or recordings. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/mxr185/index.html); [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/sample-packs/mxr-185/)

### Inferences
- The Dolby track is probably the SDS-V: 1982 is during the SDS-V's production years (1981 to end of 1983), and Wikipedia cites the same Mix article for SDS-V use. Not confirmed by the article itself.

### Gaps
- No step charts or transcriptions were found for any listed track. Note that Simmons parts were usually played live on pads, not programmed.
- A Prince link to the Sakata DPM-48 appears only in an unsourced blog comment, seen in search excerpts; not verified.
- No recordings were found for the SDS 400, MXR 185, Visco, X Drum or AJK. The search budget ran out before a dedicated MXR 185 recordings search could run.

## Q8. Free, clearly licensed MIDI pattern files

### Takeaway
None found for any of these machines.

### Cited Findings
- The MXR 185 sample pack at Polynominal includes "8 stereo loop grooves at 125 BPM". These are audio loops in a sample product, not MIDI, and no licence terms were seen. — [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/sample-packs/mxr-185/)

### Inferences
- The SDS-V and SDS 400 were played from pads (or an external SDS6), and the MXR and Sakata stored patterns only in user memory or on tape and cartridge. Machine-specific "factory" MIDI therefore cannot exist; any MIDI would have to be generic.

### Gaps
- The web-search budget was used up before a dedicated MIDI-file search, so this is a weak negative, not a confirmed absence.
