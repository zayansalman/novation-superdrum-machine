# Korg drum machines and synths: hardware setup, pack contents, presets and famous uses (DDM-110, DDM-220, KPR-77, KR-55, "KRZ", M1, Mini Pops, Poly-800, T3)

_Companion data file: `korg.json` (same folder). Pack facts below come from a read-only listing and header/spectrum scan of `~/Splice/sounds/packs/<folder>`; nothing was played or changed. The web-search quota for this session ran out partway through, so several follow-up searches (listed under Gaps) were never run._

## 1. What voices does each machine have, in front-panel or key order, with the exact labels?

### Takeaway
Printed labels are confirmed for the DDM-110 (owner's manual panel) and the KPR-77 (Korg's own programming sheet). The M1 and T3 have official numbered ROM drum lists (44 and 85 sounds). For the KR-55, DDM-220 and Mini Pops 7, the names come from reviews, not panel photos. "Korg KRZ" could not be tied to any real Korg product.

### Cited Findings
- **DDM-110 Super Drums.** There are ten number keys, read left to right: ACCENT, BASS, SNARE, RIM, HI TOM, LO TOM, CLOSED HH, OPEN HH, CYMBAL, CLAPS(TRIG). These are taken from the panel drawing on the cover and p.6 of the owner's manual. The manual lists the nine sounds as: 1 Bass drum, 2 Snare drum, 3 Rimshot, 4 High tom, 5 Low tom, 6 Closed hi-hat, 7 Open hi-hat, 8 Cymbal (crash), 9 Handclaps (p.12). The manual says these are "PCM digital recordings of the real thing". — [Korg DDM-110 Owner's Manual](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/371/3751/)
- **DDM-220 Super Percussion.** It has nine PCM Latin sounds: Hi Conga, Lo Conga, Timbale, Wood Block, Cowbell, Hi & Low Agogo (two sounds), Cabasa, Tambourine. This list comes from Trish McGrath's review in Electronics & Music Maker, Oct 1984, given left to right. — [E&MM review via muzines](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/korg-ddm220/1468). A search snippet from soundprogramming.net gives "10 pads" and "9 8-bit samples at 15.6 kHz". — [soundprogramming.net](https://soundprogramming.net/drum-machines/korg/korg-ddm-220-super-percussion)
- **KPR-77.** Korg's blank programming sheet lists the instrument rows top to bottom as: ACCENT, SYM/CLPS, OPEN HH, CLOSED HH, SNARE DRUM, HIGH TOM/FLAM, LOW TOM/FLAM, BASS DRUM. The factory charts shorten these to ACC, CY, CLPS, OH, CH (or HH), SD, HT/HTF, LT/LTF, BD. — [Korg KPR-77 Rhythm Charts (PDF)](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/129/2119/). Vintage Synth Explorer lists the sounds as "bass, snare, hi/low toms, open and closed hi-hats, accent and (switchable) cymbal/clap", with 6-voice polyphony, released in 1983. — [Vintage Synth Explorer](https://www.vintagesynth.com/korg/kpr-77). Polynominal counts 8 analog sounds, up to 6 channels and 7 mixer sliders. — [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/korg-kpr77/index.html)
- **KR-55 (Rhythm 55, 1979).** It has 12 sounds: Bass Drum, Snare, Hi-Hat 1, Hi-Hat 2, Cymbal, Rim, Low Conga, High Conga, Claves, Tom 1, Tom 2, Cow Bell. It has no claps. — [Bonedo](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/vintage-drum-machine-korg-kr-55-rhythm-55/). From the circuit side, Electric Druid names the voices Bass Drum, Snare Drum, Cymbal, Open and Closed Hi-Hat, "Low Conga / Bass Tom", "High Conga / Tom Tom", Rimshot, Claves and Cowbell. — [Electric Druid](https://electricdruid.net/?p=72032)
- **Mini Pops 7.** Wikipedia (citing Aerozone JMJ) says it has 15 drum sounds and 20 patterns and dates it to 1966. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops). Hollow Sun names the bass drum, snare drum and cymbal, and its free MP7 sample set has 16 sounds. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/minipops/index.html). Aerozone JMJ names the Quijada, Guiro and Tambourine, each with its own knob. — [Aerozone JMJ](http://aerozonejmj.fr/korg-mini-pops-7/)
- **Other Mini Pops models (Wikipedia).** Mini Pops 3 (1967, 4 sounds), Mini Pops 5 (1966), Mini Pops 20 S (1967, stereo), Mini Pops 35 (1976, 6 sounds, 9 patterns), Mini Pops 120 (1976, 6 sounds, 16 patterns, also sold as Univox), Mini Pops Junior (1972, 10 rhythms). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops)
- **M1.** There are 44 ROM drum sounds, numbered 01–44. The full list is printed on the back cover of the manual (see Q5). — [Korg M1 Owner's Manual](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/)
- **T1/T2/T3.** There are 85 ROM drum sounds, numbered 01–85. Numbers 01–44 match the M1 list in name and order (see Q5). — [T1/T2/T3 Reference Guide](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)
- **Poly-800.** This is a synth with no drum voices. It has 8 voices, one DCO each, offering saw and square waves plus white noise, and one resonant low-pass filter shared by all voices. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Poly-800)

### Inferences
- For the DDM-110, ACCENT is a key, not a sound. In a Drum Rack, map the nine sounds in key order BASS→CLAPS and turn the accent into velocity.
- For the KR-55, "Hi-Hat 1/2" are most likely closed and open, and "Tom 1/2" most likely high and low. Electric Druid pairs the high conga with "Tom Tom" and the low conga with "Bass Tom".
- The KPR-77 has 8 sounds but only 6 channels, so some sounds must share a channel. The cymbal and claps are one of the pairs (a switch). The second pair is unknown (see Q2).

### Gaps
- The KPR-77 owner's manual could not be read (ManualsLib returned 403), so the real left-to-right key order on the front panel is unverified. The order above is the row order on the programming sheet.
- No panel photo or manual was read for the KR-55, DDM-220 or Mini Pops 7, so their exact printed labels are unverified.
- The Mini Pops 7's full list of 15 or 16 voices could not be found. Only six are named in the sources: bass drum, snare, cymbal, quijada, guiro, tambourine. The search that would have filled this in could not run because the web-search quota ran out.
- **"KRZ".** No Korg product by that name came up in three searches. See Q6.

## 2. Which sounds share one voice or cut each other off?

### Takeaway
Confirmed pairs are snare/rim and closed/open hi-hat on the DDM-110 (from the manual), cymbal/claps on the KPR-77 (one switched voice), and hi-hat choke plus switched tom/conga circuits on the KR-55 (from circuit analysis). The M1 and T3 drum kits have no choke parameter.

### Cited Findings
- **DDM-110.** The manual's "Important Note" says "you can not play snare and rimshot sounds at the same time. Nor can you play open and closed hi-hat at the same time." Snare has priority over rimshot, and closed hi-hat has priority over open hi-hat. This applies in both real-time and step entry. — [DDM-110 manual p.14](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/371/3751/)
- **DDM-110 claps and TRIG OUT.** "If anything is plugged into this jack then you will not hear the CLAPS sound." — [DDM-110 manual p.10](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/371/3751/)
- **KPR-77 cymbal and claps.** These are one switchable voice, "(switchable) cymbal/clap". — [Vintage Synth Explorer](https://www.vintagesynth.com/korg/kpr-77). Korg's sheet gives them a single row, SYM/CLPS. — [KPR-77 Rhythm Charts](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/129/2119/). Polynominal mentions a separate rear output for the "snare/claps channel". — [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/korg-kpr77/index.html)
- **KR-55 hi-hats.** Open and closed hat have separate triggers. The open hat's comparator "behaves like a flip-flop reset by the closed trigger", so a closed hit cuts the open hat. — [Electric Druid](https://electricdruid.net/?p=72032)
- **KR-55 toms and congas.** Each conga and its tom are the same Bridged-T circuit, switched by a control line, so each pair is a single either/or slot. Rimshot, claves and cowbell share two ROM outputs through a 4052 multiplexer. — [Electric Druid](https://electricdruid.net/?p=72032)
- **M1.** Each drum-kit instrument has only these parameters: Index, Inst, Key, Tune, Level, Decay, Pan. "Two or more sounds cannot be assigned to the same key". — [M1 manual p.114](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/)
- **T series.** The per-instrument parameters are the same: Inst, Inst Key, Tune, Level, Decay, Pan. — [T Reference Guide p.114](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)

### Inferences
- **Drum Rack choke groups.** DDM-110: put SNARE and RIM in one group and CLOSED HH and OPEN HH in another. KR-55: put the two hats in one group. The KR-55 tom/conga pairs never sound together in a preset, so they can sit in a choke group or simply stay on separate pads.
- **M1 and T3.** There is no exclusive-group setting, so open hats ring out after a closed hat. Leave them unchoked to stay faithful.

### Gaps
- **KPR-77.** Whether the open and closed hats share a channel is unverified.
- **DDM-220.** Channel sharing is not documented. It has 9 sounds, and its sister model's 8-channel design hints that some sounds share a channel, but which ones is unknown.
- **Mini Pops 7.** Choke behaviour is unknown.

## 3. What controls does each voice have? How does accent work? Is there flam, shuffle or per-voice tuning?

### Takeaway
None of the analog or early PCM machines has per-voice tuning. Level control is mostly shared. Accent is a step-wide flag on the DDM machines, and is disputed on the KPR-77. Flam exists only on the KPR-77 toms. The ROM workstations give each instrument tune (±1 octave in 10-cent steps), level, decay and output.

### Cited Findings
- **DDM-110 controls.** It has only MASTER, HH CYMBAL ("For adjustment of hi-hat and cymbal volume") and METRONOME volume knobs, plus COARSE and FINE tempo. — [manual p.8](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/371/3751/)
- **DDM-110 accent.** "If you put an accent in a step then all instruments played on that step will sound louder." — [manual p.14](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/371/3751/). Vintage Synth Explorer instead claims "an Accent control for each sound", which contradicts the manual. — [VSE](https://www.vintagesynth.com/korg/ddm-110)
- **DDM-110 patterns.** There are 32 patterns. Patterns 01–16 hold up to 32 steps, and 17–32 hold up to 16 steps. Resolution can be 1/16, 1/16 triplet or 1/32. There are 6 songs. — [manual pp.2, 12–13](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/371/3751/)
- **DDM-110 sound engine.** 8-bit at 15.6 kHz, 8-voice polyphony, no MIDI (DIN sync only). — [VSE](https://www.vintagesynth.com/korg/ddm-110)
- **DDM-110 sync.** It syncs at 48 ppqn, so Roland 24 ppqn gear runs at double speed when the DDM is master. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/vintage/korg_ddm/index.html)
- **DDM-220 controls and accent.** Its volume controls are Master, Metronome and Cabasa/Tambourine, with "no per-voice level". A "fixed-level Accent can be placed on any step". Trig Out fires on Tambourine steps.
- **DDM-220 patterns and I/O.** It has 32 patterns of 1–2 bars, with the same step limits and resolutions as the DDM-110. It holds 6 songs and 390 bars in total, and has DIN Sync In/Out, a Start/Stop footswitch and a tape interface. — [E&MM Oct 1984](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/korg-ddm220/1468)
- **KPR-77 accent.** Korg's sheet has a separate ACCENT row, and almost every factory chart has an ACC row. — [Rhythm Charts](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/129/2119/). Polynominal says "accent for each instrument" and "flam repeat on tom 1 and tom 2". — [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/korg-kpr77/index.html). A manual excerpt on Scribd says to use the ACCENT pads while playing an instrument to hear the accent on that sound. — [Scribd (search snippet)](https://www.scribd.com/document/634906521/korg-kpr77-manual)
- **KPR-77 flam.** The sheet rows are named "HIGH TOM/FLAM" and "LOW TOM/FLAM". The charts mark flammed hits as HTF and LTF (patterns A-4 and A-13). — [Rhythm Charts](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/129/2119/)
- **KPR-77 patterns and timing.** It has 48 patterns of 32 steps, and resolutions of 1/16, 1/16 triplet, 1/32 and 1/32 triplet. Polynominal gives 6 chain banks of 256 measures, a tempo range of 35–208 BPM and two +5 V trigger outs. — [Polynominal](https://www.polynominal.com/korg-kpr77/index.html). VSE instead says "two songs", gives "level per drum part" with sliders, and says the trigger outs come from the toms. — [VSE](https://www.vintagesynth.com/korg/kpr-77)
- **KR-55 controls.** It has 6 volume knobs: Bass Drum, Snare, Cymbal, Hi-Hat, Tom/Conga, Rim/CowBell/Claves. The trigger out can be switched to whole, half, quarter, eighth or sixteenth notes, or to the bass drum figure. — [Bonedo](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/vintage-drum-machine-korg-kr-55-rhythm-55/)
- **KR-55 tempo and swing.** Tempo runs from 38 to 380 bpm. Swing patterns swing automatically, and a switch set to "All Rhythms" changes that. — [Electric Druid](https://electricdruid.net/?p=72032)
- **KR-55 accent.** None of Bonedo, Electric Druid or Cherry Audio mentions an accent.
- **Mini Pops 7 controls.** It has top-panel tempo, a cymbal level slider and a slider for the balance between bass and snare drum. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/minipops/index.html). It also has volume, balance and tempo knobs, knobs for the quijada, guiro and tambourine, and a start/stop foot pedal. — [Aerozone JMJ](http://aerozonejmj.fr/korg-mini-pops-7/)
- **M1 drum-kit parameters.** Each instrument has: Inst ---/01–44; Key C0–G8; Tune −120~+120, meaning a pitch shift "within ±1 octave" in 10-cent steps; Level; Decay, relative to the Program's VDA EG decay; and Pan, which picks the output (A, A:B 9:1–1:9, B, C, C+D, D). The parameter table gives Level and Decay as −99~+99, but the text says "-50 to +50". — [M1 manual pp.114–115](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/)
- **T-series drum-kit parameters.** Index 0–59; Inst --/01–85; Inst Key C0–G8; Inst Tune −120~+120 (10-cent steps, ±1 octave); Inst Level −99~+99; Inst Decay −99~+99; Pan A, 9:1–1:9, B, C, C+D, D. — [T Reference Guide p.114](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)
- **Poly-800 envelopes.** It has three six-stage envelopes (Attack, Decay, Break Point, Slope, Sustain, Release), with each DCO's level controlled by its own envelope. It has one triangle LFO, worked from the joystick. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Poly-800). The noise generator shares DEG3 with the filter. — [E&SM, July 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/customise/3865)

### Inferences
- **DDM-110/220 in a Drum Rack.** Use one fixed velocity for normal hits and a second fixed, higher velocity for accented steps, applied to every voice on that step. Leave the pads untuned.
- **KR-55 swing.** The swing switch suggests that swing rhythms should be clipped with swing applied, and straight rhythms without it. The amount of swing is unknown.

### Gaps
- The KPR-77 accent behaviour (per instrument or per step) is unresolved without the owner's manual.
- Whether the KPR-77 had shuffle is not documented.
- The swing amount on the KR-55 and Mini Pops is unknown.

## 4. Any built-in effects? What do they apply to?

### Takeaway
The DDM-110, DDM-220, KPR-77, KR-55 and Mini Pops have no effects. The Poly-800 has a chorus on its whole output. The M1 and T3 each have two multi-effect processors with 33 types. Drum sounds go through the effects or around them depending on each instrument's Pan (output) setting.

### Cited Findings
- **The five drum machines.** None of the sources for the DDM-110/220, KPR-77, KR-55 or Mini Pops mention any effect. That covers the DDM-110 manual, the E&MM review, VSE, Polynominal, Bonedo, Electric Druid and Hollow Sun.
- **M1 effect section.** It is a "two-system, two-channel Multi Digital Effect unit… 2 effects and 2 panpots with a 4-input (A, B, C and D) and 4-output (1/L, 2/R, 3 and 4) configuration".
- **M1 serial placement.** Inputs A and B go through Effect 1 and then Effect 2 to 1/L and 2/R. C and D go to outputs 3 and 4 "unprocessed", but can be mixed into Effect 2 through PAN3 and PAN4.
- **M1 parallel placement.** Effect 1 processes A and B, and Effect 2 processes C and D.
- **M1 effect types.** Types 1–25 are stereo and 26–33 are dual. "Instrument Pan (Global F 4) is available for Programs of the drum kit." — [M1 manual pp.36–37](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/)
- **M1 effect families.** Reverb, Early Reflection, Delay, Chorus, Flanger, Phase Shift, Tremolo, Equalizer, Overdrive, Exciter, Ensemble, Rotary Speaker and Combination types. — [M1 manual contents p.2](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/)
- **T series effects.** It has "two digital multi-effect units". The SysEx parameter table lists 33 types. — [T Reference Guide pp.150, 154](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)
  - 1–3 Hall, 4–5 Room, 6 Live Stage
  - 7–9 Early Reflection 1–3
  - 10 Stereo Delay, 11 Cross Delay
  - 12–13 Stereo Chorus, 14–15 Flanger
  - 16–17 Phase Shifter, 18–19 Stereo Tremolo
  - 20 3 Band EQ, 21 Overdrive, 22 Distortion, 23 Exciter
  - 24 Symphonic Ensemble, 25 Rotary Speaker
  - 26–33 Delay combined with Hall, Room, Early Reflection, Delay, Chorus, Flanger, Phaser or Tremolo
- **T-series drum routing.** "If the drum sound is assigned to C, C+D, or D, and the Program mode setting Effect Placement of the Program is turned off, there will be no sound from 1/L, 2/R, or the headphones." — [T Reference Guide p.115](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)
- **Poly-800.** It has a built-in chorus. The 1986 Poly-800 II replaced it with a digital delay. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Poly-800)

### Inferences
- For a faithful M1 or T3 kit, put a reverb or delay return on the Drum Rack and send only the pads that would have been panned to A/B. Pads set to C or D would bypass Effect 1 on the hardware.

### Gaps
- The factory effect settings of the M1 and T drum programs (for example T "A09 M1 Drums" and "B09 T1 Drums") were not documented in the pages read.

## 5. For the ROM machines (M1, T3): drum kit key maps, total drum sounds, the official ordered list and default MIDI note map. For synths without drums, how are drum sounds made?

### Takeaway
The M1 has 44 drum sounds and 4 user drum kits of up to 30 keys each. The T series has 85 sounds (the M1's 44 plus 41 new ones) and 4 kits of up to 60 keys each. Neither manual publishes the factory kit key maps. The only key map shown is an example screen in the T Reference Guide. On the Poly-800, drums can only be faked with patches, and its period magazine coverage calls this hard.

### Cited Findings
- **M1 kit structure.** "Up to 30 kinds of drum sounds can be assigned to each of the 4 Drum Kits (1 to 4)" (Index 0–29). "Any sound assigned to a key will also occupy all unassigned keys beneath it, or up to the next assigned key." The drum list is on the back cover. Specs: PCM 2 Mword (4 MB), 16-bit; 16 voices; outputs 1/L, 2/R, 3, 4. — [M1 manual pp.114, 134](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/)
- **M1 official drum sound list (back cover).** 01 Kick 1, 02 Kick 2, 03 Kick 3, 04 Snare 1, 05 Snare 2, 06 Snare 3, 07 Snare 4, 08 Side Stick, 09 Tom 1, 10 Tom 2, 11 Closed HH 1, 12 Open HH 1, 13 Closed HH 2, 14 Open HH 2, 15 Crash, 16 Conga 1, 17 Conga 2, 18 Timbales 1, 19 Timbales 2, 20 Cowbell, 21 Claps, 22 Tambourine, 23 E. Tom, 24 Ride, 25 Rap, 26 Whip, 27 Shaker, 28 Pole, 29 Block, 30 FingerSnap, 31 Drop, 32 Vibe Hit, 33 Hammer, 34 Metal Hit, 35 Pluck, 36 FlexaTone, 37 Wind Bell, 38 Tubular 1, 39 Tubular 2, 40 Tubular 3, 41 Tubular 4, 42 Bell Ring, 43 Metronome 1, 44 Metronome 2. — [M1 manual back cover](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/)
- **T-series kit structure.** "Up to 60 types of drum sound can be assigned to each of 4 Drum Kits (1 – 4)." Keys without a sound "will automatically be given the sound assigned to the next higher key". — [T Reference Guide pp.114–115](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/). Note this is the opposite direction to the M1 manual's wording.
- **T-series hardware specs.** T3: 61 keys; T1/T2/T3 have 16 voices and 16-bit samples; T2/T3 have 4 Mword (8 MB) of PCM ROM; 200 programs and 100 combinations; 3.5-inch floppy. — [T Reference Guide p.154](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/). soundprogramming.net gives 1989, "190 waveforms, 85 drum sounds". — [soundprogramming.net](https://soundprogramming.net/synthesizers/korg/korg-t3/)
- **T-series official drum sound list (back cover).**
  - 01–44: same as the M1 list, spelled Closed HH1, Open HH1, Closed HH2, Open HH2 and Metronome1, Metronome2.
  - 45–62: 45 Pro BD, 46 Tight BD, 47 Punch BD, 48 Synth BD, 49 Pro SD 1, 50 Pro SD 2, 51 Tight SD, 52 Ambient SD, 53 Synth SD, 54 Rim Shot, 55 Stick Hit, 56 AmbientTom, 57 Closed HH3, 58 Open HH3, 59 Pedal HH, 60 Clang Hit, 61 Bell Ride, 62 Ping Ride.
  - 63–71: 63 Bongo Low, 64 Bongo High, 65 Bongo Slap, 66 Claps 2, 67 Maracas 1, 68 Maracas 2, 69 Cabasa, 70 Block 2, 71 Bell Hit.
  - 72–85: 72 Techno Zap, 73 Marimba, 74 Gamelan 1, 75 Gamelan 2, 76 Potcover, 77 Cymbell, 78 Timpani, 79 Clicker 1, 80 Clicker 2, 81 Spectrum4L, 82 Spectrum4H, 83 Noise, 84 Perc.WaveL, 85 Perc.WaveH.
  - Source: [T Reference Guide back cover](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)
- **T-series example key map.** The example "GLOBAL DRUM KIT1" screen shows #00 Inst 01 (Kick 1) on C1, #01 02 on D1, #02 03 on E1, #03 04 (Snare 1) on F1, #04 05 on G1 and #05 06 on A1. All are at Tune +000, Decay +00 and Pan 5:5; level is +00 except #02 at +12. — [T Reference Guide p.114](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)
- **MIDI note ranges.** The M1 sends notes 24–108. — [M1 manual p.135](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/139/1685/). The T3 sends 24–108, the T2 16–115 and the T1 9–120. — [T Reference Guide p.156](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/3574/)
- **Factory T-series drum programs.** Programs whose names point to drum kits include A09 M1 Drums, A49 Pitch Kit, B09 T1 Drums, B29 Drum Kit 2, B39 Tuned Drum, B49 Gate Drum and B99 TunedDrum2. — [T1/T2/T3 Voice Name List](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/207/1556/)
- **Poly-800 drums.** You "can't synthesise drums because you need to take the filter resonance down to smooth out the bass from the DCO's", which also removes the enveloped noise burst. The noise generator shares DEG3 with the filter. The percussive Voice 81 takes the filter out of the signal path so DEG3 controls the noise. In that patch DCO1 hits a high level only briefly for the attack, DCO2 gives a bright top, and a simple AR envelope shapes the noise burst. — [Electronic Soundmaker, "Customise" Part 1, July 1985](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/customise/3865)
- **Poly-800 filter.** It is one resonant low-pass filter shared by all voices ("technically a paraphonic synth"). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Poly-800)

### Inferences
- **Note numbering.** A 61-key board sending 24–108 with ±1-octave shift implies the lowest key, C2, is MIDI 36. That gives Korg's convention as C4 = 60, so C1 = 24 and C0–G8 = 12–115. The midi_note values in the JSON for the T example screen use this convention.
- **Drum Rack layout.** With no published factory map, lay M1 and T3 sounds out in ROM order. The T-series kits can also host M1 kits unchanged, since sounds 01–44 are the same.
- **Poly-800 kicks.** These were probably made from a low DCO with a fast envelope decay, because the manual documents no pitch envelope and the filter is shared. This is inferred, not sourced.

### Gaps
- The factory key maps of M1 Drum Kits 1–4 and T Drum Kits 1–4 are not in either manual. They might be recoverable from a factory SysEx or data dump, which this research did not do.
- No sourced Poly-800 kick patch with settings was found. The E&SM piece gives settings only for a percussive pad, Voice 81.

## 6. What does each pack folder contain? (naming, gaps, extras, loops; identifying "KRZ" and the Mini Pops model)

### Takeaway
Every folder holds 44.1 kHz/16-bit one-shots with no loops. Only these are complete: the M1 (all 44 ROM sounds), the DDM-110 (two full 9-voice sets), the DDM-220 (9 of 9) and "Korg KPR77" (8 of 8). The T3 pack is a partial, band-limited set with one empty file. Neither the "KRZ" pack nor the Mini Pops model could be identified.

### Cited Findings
These facts come from the read-only `ls -R` listing and header/spectrum scan of `~/Splice/sounds/packs`. They are local observations, not web sources.

- **Korg DDM110 (18 files).** Set A is mono and named by voice: Ddm110Kick, Ddm110Snare, Ddm110Rimshot, Ddm110Tom_Hi, Ddm110Tom_Lo, Ddm110Hat_C, Ddm110Hat_O, Ddm110Cymbal, Ddm110Clap (0.07–0.70 s). - **Korg M1 (44 stereo files in Bassdrums/Snaredrums/Cymbals/Percussion/Misc).**
  - These match the 44 ROM drum sounds one-for-one. Misspellings: Sideststick, Tubalar Bell-01..04, Flexttone, Belrng, Cowbel.

- **Korg Minipops (30 stereo files in BASSDRUM/SNARES/CYMBALS/MISC).**

  - MISC holds Tom-01/-02, which are only 21–27 ms long, and Woodblock-01/-02.
  - No model name appears anywhere in the folder.
- **Korg Poly-800 (4 stereo kicks).** Bassdrum-01 (0.18 s), -02 (0.55 s), -03 (1.96 s), -04 (0.33 s).

  - "Rim shot.wav" is a 44-byte header with **0 audio bytes**, so it is empty.
  - There are no toms, crash or ride.
- **Duplicates.** Hashing the audio data found no duplicates between these folders, or between them and any other pack in the Splice packs directory.
- **Mini Pops models for comparison.** The MP-7 has 15 sounds and 20 rhythms; Hollow Sun's MP7 set has 16 sounds, including guiro, quijada and tambourine per Aerozone. The MP-35 and MP-120 have 6 sounds each. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops); [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/minipops/index.html); [Aerozone JMJ](http://aerozonejmj.fr/korg-mini-pops-7/)
- **"KRZ".** Searches for "Korg KRZ" returned nothing about any such product.

### Inferences
- **Bandwidth.** Measured from the 99.9% energy roll-off of the pack files:
  - **KRZ** has almost nothing above ~12.4 kHz. That fits a source sampled at roughly 25 kHz, which would point to a mid-1980s digital ROM machine rather than an analog box.
  - **T3** files stop at ~8.3 kHz. That points to a low-rate capture, and the set may not be faithful to the hardware's real bandwidth.
  - **M1** cymbals reach ~13.5 kHz.
  - **DDM-110** cymbals and hats show energy up to ~21.9 kHz, even though the reported source is 15.6 kHz. That is consistent with imaging above the original Nyquist.
- **"KRZ" identity.** It is most likely a mislabel. One untested idea is "KRZ" as shorthand for Kurzweil. Treat it as unknown.
- **Mini Pops model.** Seven kick variants, thirteen snare variants and no guiro, quijada or tambourine do not match a single MP-7 one-shot set. The pack may have been recorded from patterns or several units, so the model is unknown.
- **Which tom is which.** DDM-110 set B: Tom-01 is higher than Tom-02 by spectral roll-off. KRZ: Tom-01 is the darker of the two.
- **Out of scope.** The packs directory also has folders named "Rhythm 33" and "Rhythm 77" that might be Korg rhythm boxes. They were not examined.

### Gaps
- Which ROM numbers the T3 pack files come from cannot be told from names alone. That includes Bell01/02, Hit, Click, Blocks, Shaker-01/02, Stick and Bassdrum/Snaredrum 01–05.
- The KR-55 pack's Conga and Tom (high or low) are unknown.
- The checks that would have tested the Kurzweil idea for "KRZ" could not run because the search quota ran out.

## 7. Factory preset patterns: names, and step grids where a source shows them

### Takeaway
The KPR-77 shipped with 48 preprogrammed rhythms, and Korg's own chart prints them in drum notation, not grids. The KR-55's 48 preset names are known. Only partial preset names exist for the Mini Pops 7. The only published DDM-110 step chart is the manual's example pattern, which is transcribed in the JSON with a caution. No KR-55 or Mini Pops step grids were found.

### Cited Findings
- **KPR-77 Rhythm Charts.** The title reads "Preprogrammed rhythm patterns". "A-1 represents the Basic Bank 1 of Group A." The patterns run A-1…A-16, B-1…B-16 and C-1…C-16, and A-11 is blank. Korg recommends "the reading 6-8 on the dial for the Tempo Knob". Each pattern is printed as staff notation per instrument row, for example A-1 uses ACC, SD, LT, BD and A-16 uses ACC, CY, OH, CH, SD, HT, LT, BD. The JSON lists the rows each pattern uses. — [Korg KPR-77 Rhythm Charts](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/129/2119/)
- **KR-55 preset names (Bonedo).**
  - 01 Foxtrot, 02 Shuffle, 03 March, 04 Waltz, 05 Jazz Waltz, 06 Rock Waltz
  - 07–09 Swing 1–3, 10–12 Ballad 1–3, 13–15 Bossa Nova 1–3, 16–18 5/4 Beat 1–3
  - 19 Cha Cha, 20 Samba, 21 Afro, 22 Mambo, 23 Rumba, 24 Beguine, 25 Enka, 26 Tango, 27 Habanera
  - 28–30 Pops 1–3, 31–42 Rock 1–12, 43–48 Disco 1–6
  - There are 16 intros and 16 fill-ins. The KR-55B has 96, 32 and 32. — [Bonedo](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/vintage-drum-machine-korg-kr-55-rhythm-55/)
- **KR-55 rhythm selection.** "Each of the black pattern select buttons selects three rhythm patterns" through A/B/C, and intros and fills are one bar long. Foxtrot, Shuffle and March share one intro and fill. Waltz (3/4), Ballad (6/8) and 5/4 patterns are grouped together. — [Cherry Audio KR-55C docs](https://docs.cherryaudio.com/cherry-audio/instruments/kr-55c/patt-mode-pres)
- **KR-55 conflicting count.** Wikipedia instead says "48 presets = 16 patterns + 16 intros + 16 fill-ins". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_KR_55)
- **Mini Pops preset names.**
  - Hollow Sun: cha-cha, beguine, tango, rhumba, bossa-nova, waltz, march, rock "and so on", picked with push buttons plus a bank-select toggle. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/minipops/index.html)
  - KVR: BEGUINE and SLOW ROCK. — [KVR POPS-7](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/pops-7-by-forgotten-keys)
  - Wikipedia: "Rock 1". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops)
  - A search snippet adds JAZZ WALTZ, MERINGUE and HABANERA from an unidentified listing (unverified).
  - The original MP-7 had 32 steps per pattern. — [Bucket Pops manual (search snippet)](https://manualmachine.com/fullbucketmusic/bucketpops/26866459-manual-v1)
- **DDM-110 factory content.** The machine ships with factory-loaded demo patterns and a demo song, also on cassette, but the manual does not chart them.
- **DDM-110 example step chart (p.15).** "Pattern number = 01 (resolution 1/16 : time = 8/4)" uses rows OH, CH, SD, CLPS, BD. — [DDM-110 manual pp.11, 15](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/1/371/3751/). Transcription, two bars of 4/4 at 16 steps per bar:
  - OPEN HH: `..........................x.....`
  - CLOSED HH: `xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx...x.......x.`
  - SNARE: `....x.......x.........x.......x.`
  - CLAPS: `....x.......x..x.xx.x.........xx` (the clap at step 21 vs 20 is uncertain in the scan)
  - BASS: `x.......x......x.xx...x........x`
  - There is no accent in this example.

### Inferences
- The KR-55's 48 names in numeric order line up with 16 buttons × A/B/C:
  - Button 1 = Foxtrot/Shuffle/March, button 2 = the waltzes, then Swing, Ballad, Bossa Nova, 5/4.
  - Then Cha Cha/Samba/Afro, Mambo/Rumba/Beguine, Enka/Tango/Habanera, Pops.
  - Then Rock 1–12 on four buttons and Disco 1–6 on two buttons.
  - This is inferred from Bonedo's numbering plus Cherry Audio's note that the three patterns on a button share an intro and fill.
- The KPR-77 charts could be turned into 16-step grids from the PDF, but that needs careful reading of the staff notation at full resolution. They were not converted here, to avoid misreading rests and beams.

### Gaps
- No published step grids were found for KR-55 or Mini Pops presets. The full ordered list of the 20 MP-7 rhythms was also not found.
- The DDM-110 demo song and patterns are not charted anywhere found.

## 8. Famous recordings that verifiably used each machine (with published step charts)

### Takeaway
The only well-evidenced uses are the Mini Pops 7 on Jean-Michel Jarre's Oxygène and Équinoxe, plus Aphex Twin and Echo & the Bunnymen, and the KR-55 snare on Depeche Mode's Speak & Spell. No verified recordings were found for the DDM-110, DDM-220, KPR-77, M1 drum kits, T3 or Poly-800 drums. No published step chart was found for any famous groove.

### Cited Findings
- **Jean-Michel Jarre, Oxygène (1976), Mini Pops 7.**
  - The MP7 was "the rhythmic driving force" of the album. Jarre combined patterns by pressing two or more preset buttons at once, overlaying triplet patterns on 4/4. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/minipops/index.html)
  - It supplied the rhythms for Parts 2, 4 and 6. — [Aerozone JMJ](http://aerozonejmj.fr/korg-mini-pops-7/)
  - "Combining BEGUINE with SLOW ROCK gets the classic Oxygene IV rhythm." — [KVR POPS-7](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/pops-7-by-forgotten-keys)
  - Wikipedia agrees, citing Hollow Sun. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops)
  - **Grid:** none published in an accessible form. A Captain Pikant Patreon post (Aug 2024) links a PDF transcription of the Oxygène IV pattern, but it could not be opened or verified. — [Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/posts/110158905)
- **Jean-Michel Jarre, Équinoxe Parts V–VII (1978).** Said to use the "Rock 1" pattern; Wikipedia marks this as citation needed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops). Bonedo instead credits Équinoxe to the KR-55, which conflicts with the KR-55's 1979 release date. — [Bonedo](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/vintage-drum-machine-korg-kr-55-rhythm-55/)
- **Aphex Twin, "minipops 67 [120.2]" (Syro, 2014).** Wikipedia, citing FACT Magazine, says the Mini Pops was used on the album and the title is a tribute. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops)
- **Echo & the Bunnymen, "The Pictures on My Wall" (1979).** Recorded with a Mini Pops Junior, per One Two Testing, Dec 1985, cited by Wikipedia. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korg_Mini_Pops)
- **R.E.M. "Everybody Hurts" and OMD "Enola Gay".** Hollow Sun claims these used a Mini Pops, without evidence. — [Hollow Sun](https://hollowsun.com/nostalgia/contributors/minipops/index.html)
- **Depeche Mode, Speak & Spell (1981), KR-55.**
  - A write-up of Alex Ball's reconstruction video lists a "KR-55 Snare" segment and credits the kick to an ARP 2600. — [Synthtopia](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2023/11/03/the-drum-sounds-of-classic-depeche-mode/)
  - A fan sample-source wiki says the snare heard throughout "Just Can't Get Enough" comes from the KR-55. This was seen only in a search snippet; the page returned 403. — [DM Live wiki](https://dmlive.wiki/wiki/List_of_Depeche_Mode_sample_sources_by_album/Speak_%26_Spell)
  - Bonedo also names Speak & Spell. — [Bonedo](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/vintage-drum-machine-korg-kr-55-rhythm-55/)
  - A forum quote of Vince Clarke, seen only in a search summary, says only the snare and hi-hat came from a KR-55 preset unit. Treat it as secondhand.
- **KPR-77.** Synthpedia claims use by Prince, Depeche Mode and The Cure but names no tracks (search snippet). — [Synthpedia](https://synthpedia.net/korg/kpr-77)

### Inferences
- For Oxygène IV, the closest sourced recipe is to layer the Mini Pops BEGUINE and SLOW ROCK presets. Without a grid, an honest clip would need to be transcribed by ear from the record. That was not done here.

### Gaps
- No verified famous recording was found that is specifically credited to the DDM-110, DDM-220, KPR-77, M1 drum kits, T3 or Poly-800 drums. Search summaries tie the M1's fame to its piano and Organ 2 patches, not its drums.
- No published, freely viewable step chart was found for any of the grooves above.

## 9. Free, clearly licensed MIDI pattern files for these machines

### Takeaway
None were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Cherry Audio KR-55C.** Its emulation includes all KR-55 and KR-55B rhythms, but it is a commercial plugin, not a MIDI file set. — [Cherry Audio docs](https://docs.cherryaudio.com/cherry-audio/instruments/kr-55c/patt-mode-pres)
- **Forgotten Keys POPS-7.** It recreates the original Mini Pops rhythm patterns and has a MIDI file recorder. It is a commercial product. — [KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/pops-7-by-forgotten-keys)
- **Matrixsynth MP-7 demo.** A post demonstrates all Mini Pops 7 rhythm patterns as audio, not MIDI (search snippet). — [Matrixsynth](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2024/10/korg-mini-pops-7-all-rhythm-pattern.html)

### Inferences
- The most practical license-clean route to MIDI is to convert Korg's own KPR-77 charts and the DDM-110 manual example into clips. Both are in the manufacturer's documentation.

- A dedicated search for free MIDI pattern files could not run because the session's web-search quota ran out.
